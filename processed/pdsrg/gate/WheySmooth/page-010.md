Figures 2 and 3 also show the AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) role as an energy sensor, as AMPK activation
suppresses MPS. Skeletal muscle during exercise increases the use of ATP (turnover may increase >100 fold) resulting
in the accumulation of adenosine monophosphate (AMP) thus affecting the cellular AMP/ATP ratio causing the
activation of AMPK.175 Therefore AMPK is a sensor of intracellular energy status and works to maintain stores for cell
survival by regulating anabolic and catabolic pathways including MPS and muscle protein breakdown (MPB) as
necessary. Further, exercise intensity and duration regulates different AMPK heterotrimer complexes leading to
different functional responses.176 In short, mTORC1 regulates skeletal muscle by controlling protein translation
initiation through its two major downstream targets: p70 ribosomal S6K and the eukaryotic initiation factor 4E binding
protein 1 (4E-BP1).177 mTORC1 activity is regulated by energy sensing AMPK through phosphorylation of tuberous
sclerosis complex 2 (TSC2) at Thr1227 or Ser1345, which improves the ability of TSC2 to inhibit mTOR activity as
necessary.4,176,178 The interplay between mTORC1 and AMPK is tightly regulated to support maintaining exercise
