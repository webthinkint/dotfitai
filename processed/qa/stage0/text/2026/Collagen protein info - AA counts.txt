Why collagen is still valuable
Although collagen is a poor protein for stimulating skeletal muscle protein synthesis because it lacks tryptophan and is very low in leucine and several other EAAs, it is uniquely rich in amino acids that support connective tissue:
Glycine: ~21–22% 
Proline: ~12–13% 
Hydroxyproline: ~11–13% 
These amino acids are the primary structural building blocks of collagen in:
Tendons 
Ligaments 
Cartilage 
Skin 
Bone matrix 
This is why collagen peptides are best viewed as a specialized connective tissue support protein, not a replacement for high-quality complete proteins such as whey, milk, egg, soy, or beef when the goal is maximizing muscle protein synthesis.

Key Trainer Takeaways
Whey contains approximately three times more essential amino acids (EAAs) than collagen on a gram-for-gram basis. 
Leucine, the primary trigger for muscle protein synthesis, is nearly 4 times higher in whey (110 mg/g vs. 28 mg/g). 
Collagen contains no tryptophan, making it an incomplete protein that cannot independently support optimal whole-body protein synthesis. 
Collagen's strength lies in its exceptionally high content of glycine, proline, and hydroxyproline, making it ideal for supporting tendons, ligaments, cartilage, skin, and other connective tissues. 
For athletes and active adults, the two proteins are complementary rather than competitive: whey is the superior choice for building and maintaining muscle, while collagen provides specialized support for connective tissue health and recovery.
