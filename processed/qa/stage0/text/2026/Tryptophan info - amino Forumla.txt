How much tryptophan does the body need daily to maintain to maintain normal protein synthesis - i.e., daily gram or milligram requirement
The daily requirement for tryptophan to support normal protein synthesis and general physiological needs is actually quite small compared with what is used in research for sleep or mood.
Daily Requirement
The current recommendation from the World Health Organization, Food and Agriculture Organization, and United Nations University is:
Tryptophan: 4 mg/kg body weight/day 
This is the estimated requirement to maintain nitrogen balance and normal protein synthesis in healthy adults.
What this means in practical terms
| Body Weight | Daily Requirement |
| 50 kg (110 lb) | 200 mg/day |
| 60 kg (132 lb) | 240 mg/day |
| 70 kg (154 lb) | 280 mg/day |
| 80 kg (176 lb) | 320 mg/day |
| 90 kg (198 lb) | 360 mg/day |
| 100 kg (220 lb) | 400 mg/day |
For most adults, the requirement is therefore only about 250–400 mg/day.
Why the requirement is so low
The 4 mg/kg recommendation is intended only to provide enough tryptophan for:
Protein synthesis 
Production of niacin (vitamin B3) 
Synthesis of serotonin 
Synthesis of melatonin 
Other normal metabolic functions 
It is not intended to maximize serotonin production, improve sleep, or enhance mood.
Typical dietary intake
Most people consuming adequate protein easily exceed this amount.
For example:
25 g whey protein: ~400–500 mg tryptophan 
3 oz chicken breast: ~300–350 mg 
2 eggs: ~160–180 mg 
1 cup Greek yogurt: ~180–220 mg 
 Someone eating 100 g of high-quality protein/day typically consumes approximately 900–1,300 mg of tryptophan/day-or ~3–5 times the minimum physiological requirement.
For comparison: protein quality
Tryptophan is one of the nine indispensable (essential) amino acids. Because it is required in relatively small amounts, it is seldom the amino acid that limits protein synthesis in animal proteins. In cereal grains, however, lysine is much more commonly the limiting amino acid than tryptophan.
Summary
Minimum adult requirement: 4 mg/kg/day 
Typical adult (80 kg): ~320 mg/day 
Typical intake from a balanced, protein-rich diet: 900–1,300 mg/day 
This requirement is sufficient for normal protein synthesis and essential metabolic functions, but it is substantially lower than the 1–3 g/day of supplemental tryptophan often studied for effects on sleep, relaxation, or mood.

how much tryptophan is in 6oz of animal protein such as beef and chicken
The exact amount depends on the cut of meat and cooking method, but 6 oz (170 g) of cooked lean animal protein generally provides well over the daily requirement for tryptophan.
Approximate tryptophan content in 6 oz (170 g) cooked
| Animal Protein | Tryptophan (mg) |
| Chicken breast (skinless) | 650–750 mg |
| Turkey breast | 700–800 mg |
| Lean beef (sirloin, top round) | 500–650 mg |
| Pork loin | 550–700 mg |
| Salmon | 600–700 mg |
| Tuna | 500–650 mg |
Comparison with the daily requirement
The adult requirement is:
4 mg/kg/day 
| Body Weight | Daily Requirement |
| 70 kg (154 lb) | 280 mg |
| 80 kg (176 lb) | 320 mg |
| 90 kg (198 lb) | 360 mg |
Therefore, 6 oz of chicken breast (≈700 mg) provides approximately:
250% of the requirement for a 70 kg adult 
220% for an 80 kg adult 
195% for a 90 kg adult 
Even 6 oz of lean beef (≈550–600 mg) provides roughly 150–200% of the daily physiological requirement.
Relative abundance in protein
Tryptophan represents approximately:
Chicken: ~1.2–1.3% of total protein 
Turkey: ~1.3–1.4% 
Beef: ~1.1–1.2% 
Fish: ~1.2–1.3% 
Since 6 oz of cooked lean meat contains approximately 45–50 g of protein, multiplying by these percentages yields about 500–750 mg of tryptophan, depending on the source.
