Yes — alcohol consumption can increase cortisol levels, especially when consumed in moderate-to-high amounts or chronically.
Alcohol acts as a physiological stressor that activates the body’s hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis, leading to increased release of cortisol, the primary “stress hormone.” Acute drinking can temporarily elevate cortisol for several hours, while chronic heavy intake may keep cortisol chronically elevated and disrupt its normal daily rhythm. Elevated cortisol from alcohol is associated with impaired recovery, poorer sleep quality, increased abdominal fat storage, muscle protein breakdown, insulin resistance, and negative effects on mood and cognitive function.
For exercisers and athletes, this is one reason excessive alcohol intake can interfere with muscle recovery, body composition goals, performance adaptation, and overall recovery capacity.
