From the Practitioner Dietary Supplement Reference Guide (PDSRG) for Performance Athletes
Carbohydrates in Refueling & Rebuilding Muscles
Protein is essential to life, and while carbohydrates are not deemed as such, they are crucial to performance. Therefore, it would be remiss to ignore dietary CHO recommendations for maximizing performance since CHO supplies most of the energy that powers muscular activity. After substantial energy/glycogen depletion, the mean hourly rate of replacement is ~5–6 mmol (or 5–6%), therefore it can take approximately 24 hours to refill stores.8,9,10, As with MPS, immediately following exercise and up to four hours,36,37,38,39,46 there is a high potential for muscle glycogen storage as a result of the depletion-activated stimulation of the glycogen synthase enzyme and exercise-induced increases in muscle membrane permeability and insulin sensitivity.54,55, This potential can be reached if CHO is consumed within this period. If not, refueling rates are significantly lower.8,9,10,46,51,55 This “window” of opportunity for glycogen storage during the early post-exercise period is part of the “metabolic window” concept (discussed below) because as with muscle protein synthesis rates, glycogen synthesis rates decrease after this time-frame even if CHO feeding is continued properly.4,8,9,10,28,46,51  Therefore,  although short-term CHO restriction may not hinder an acute performance bout and notwithstanding the above notes on CHO and fat periodization experimenting, the basic strategy for muscle repair, synthesis and refueling, including maximizing muscle liver and glycogen stores and utilization for performance athletes is as follows:
Carbohydrates Amounts and Timing1,4,8,9,10,19,28,46,51,58,,
Overall CHO intake should be between 40/50-70%, depending on activity type, duration, and intensity, of total calorie intake and spread throughout the day with focus on the pre and post exercise period. 
Most athletes’ CHO range is ~3.6–5.5 g/LB/day to maximize muscle/liver glycogen stores.
2-4 hours before exercise (depending on gastrointestinal comfort) consume ~0.5 g/lb of body weight of CHO within the pre-workout meal. 
Begin consuming 0.4-0.5 g/lb of body weight of CHO soon after exercise. Endurance athletes would continue this target each of the next four hours post exercise, before resuming the normal eating pattern. Anaerobic (e.g., off-season bodybuilders, power lifters, sprinters, etc.), intermittent (most team sports – i.e., combined intermittent aerobic and anaerobic activity such as football, soccer, basketball, baseball, rugby, hockey) athletes following the immediate post CHO and protein dose, could resume normal eating patterns (e.g., every 3-4 hours) that meet overall energy needs. 
Basic CHO recommendation ranges based on body weight and activity fuel needs: 
Low intensity activity: 1.4-2.25 g/lb/day
Moderate exercise of approximately 1 hour: 2.25-3.0 g/lb/day
Endurance-type program (1-3 hrs/day of moderate-high intensity exercise, such as team sport practices, running, etc.): 2.75-4.5 g/lb/day.
Ultra endurance program (>4-5 hrs/day of moderate-high intensity training): 3.6-5.5 g/lb/day
Pre-competition (“loading”) varies according to duration of exercise:
2.7 g/lb/d <90 min of exercise
4.5–5.5 g/lb/d >90 min of exercise
.45–1.8 g/lb final “top-off” 1–4 hours prior to event
Try to finish solid foods three hours before the event.
During exercise (for those interested in more information on types, preferences, or common usages of CHO during training/competition by endurance athletes, see Reinhard et al., “Carbohydrate Intake Practices and Determinants of Food Choices During Training in Recreational, Amateur, and Professional Endurance Athletes: A Survey Analysis”
Depending on sport, 3-6 ounces of water or sports drink every 15 minutes.
High intensity (> 70% VO2 Max) exercise >90 minutes, consume 30-60 g of CHO/hour in a 6-8% CHO-electrolyte solution (up to 120 g/hour may be most beneficial in ultra-endurance athletes that can tolerate this amount during activity).
Glucose/electrolyte solutions to maintain blood glucose levels, prevent dehydration, and reduce the immunosuppressive effects of intense exercise.
Menzies et al., found that ingesting a total of 75 g of CHO/sucrose in small 5 g doses every 5 min enhances running capacity compared with consuming the same amount of CHO in a single bolus after 75 minutes. Further, in the frequent feeding condition during the first 75 minutes, muscle glycogen was spared leading to an increased exercise capacity.

The best carbohydrates after a workout are the ones that rapidly restore muscle glycogen, improve recovery, and support muscle protein synthesis (MPS) — especially when combined with high-quality protein. The “best” choice depends on the goal, training volume, body composition goals, and how quickly another workout is coming.
Best Post-Workout Carbohydrates for Glycogen Replenishment
Fast-Digesting / High-Glycemic Carbohydrates
These are ideal immediately after intense training because they digest quickly and raise insulin efficiently to drive glucose back into muscle.
White rice 
Potatoes (especially white or mashed potatoes) 
Cream of rice 
Rice cakes 
Fruit (banana, pineapple, mango, raisins) 
Honey 
Sports drinks or glucose polymers 
Bagels or white bread 
Oatmeal (moderate-fast depending on type) 
Low-fat breakfast cereals 
These are especially useful for:
Athletes training multiple times/day 
High-volume bodybuilding or performance training 
Glycogen-depleting endurance or interval work 
Individuals struggling to recover or gain muscle 

Best Scientific Combination
The evidence consistently shows that combining carbohydrates with protein is superior to carbs alone for recovery.
A highly effective post-workout target is approximately:
0.5–0.75 g carbohydrate per lb bodyweight 
plus 
25–40 g high-quality protein 
Example for a 180-lb exerciser:
~90–135 g carbs 
30–40 g whey protein 
This combination:
Refills glycogen faster 
Enhances insulin response 
Reduces muscle breakdown 
Improves net muscle protein balance 
Accelerates recovery

| Max muscle gain/performance | Fast carbs + whey protein |
| Fat loss while preserving muscle | Moderate carbs + high protein |
| Endurance recovery | Higher total carbs + sodium/electrolytes |
| Healthy aging/longevity | Moderate clean carbs + protein blend |

Important Takeaway for Trainers
The best post-workout carbohydrate is not just about “sugar” — it’s about delivering glucose back into muscle efficiently while pairing it with sufficient amino acids to maximize recovery and muscle protein synthesis. Fast-digesting carbs work best when the goal is performance and recovery, while moderate, fiber-containing carbs may work better when appetite control and body composition are priorities.

“After training, feed the muscle fast: quality carbs refill glycogen, quality protein rebuilds muscle.”

Okay, so post-workout carbs. Here's the thing — this is actually one of the most critical windows in your entire training day, and most people either skip it or get it wrong.
The bottom line is this: if you don't get fast-acting carbs in immediately after your workout, you will NOT replenish your glycogen stores before your next training session. That's not opinion, that's just the physiology.
What works best:
1. Maltodextrin is your go-to fast-acting carb source post-workout. It exits the gastric system fast and gets into your bloodstream quickly — that speed matters here
2. Gatorade — yes, the same stuff you're already using — works for this purpose too because it uses fast-digesting sugars and maltodextrin to get carbs into your system quickly
3. A carb-to-protein ratio of roughly 2:1 is your target post-workout. So if you're getting 25g of protein, you want around 50g of carbs alongside it
4. Timing matters — start the process immediately after training, then continue to feed carbs each hour after until your stores are topped off
