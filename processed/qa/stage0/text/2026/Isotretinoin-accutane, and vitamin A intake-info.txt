MORE INFO

is it ok for a 16yr old male taking isotretinoin for acne, to continue using his multivitamin that contains vitamin A, retinol acetate at 375 mcg/1250 IU, since it's not a high amount? 
The conservative medical answer is:
Most dermatologists recommend avoiding supplemental vitamin A while taking isotretinoin (Accutane), even if the amount is relatively small.
Here's why:
Isotretinoin is a vitamin A derivative (retinoid). 
Many of the side effects of isotretinoin are essentially signs of excess retinoid exposure. 
Combining isotretinoin with additional vitamin A can theoretically increase the risk of: 
Dry skin and lips 
Headaches 
Liver stress 
Elevated triglycerides 
Joint aches 
Rarely, vitamin A toxicity symptoms 
That said, your example contains:
375 mcg retinol acetate (1,250 IU vitamin A)
which is actually a fairly modest amount. For perspective:
RDA for a 16-year-old male = about 900 mcg RAE/day (~3,000 IU) 
Upper Limit (UL) for ages 14–18 = 2,800 mcg RAE/day (~9,300 IU) 
So the amount in the multivitamin by itself is not high.
Practical Consideration
If the dermatologist specifically said:
"Avoid vitamin A supplements"
then I would discontinue the multivitamin or switch to one without preformed vitamin A during treatment.
If the multivitamin is otherwise important and contains only:
375 mcg retinol acetate 
no additional vitamin A supplements 
no cod liver oil 
many physicians would not consider that amount a major concern, but it is still worth confirming with the prescribing dermatologist because they know:
the isotretinoin dose 
treatment duration 
liver enzyme results 
triglyceride levels 
What I Usually Recommend
For someone on isotretinoin:
✅ Continue:
Multivitamin without vitamin A if available 
Vitamin D 
Minerals 
Omega-3s 
Protein supplements 
⚠️ Avoid:
Additional vitamin A supplements 
High-vitamin A liver supplements 
Cod liver oil 
Acne products containing strong retinoids unless specifically prescribed 
Bottom Line
375 mcg (1,250 IU) of vitamin A is not a large dose, and by itself is unlikely to cause toxicity in a healthy 16-year-old. However, because isotretinoin is a vitamin A derivative, the standard recommendation is to avoid supplemental vitamin A during treatment when practical. If the multivitamin is otherwise valuable, I would ask the prescribing dermatologist whether that specific amount is acceptable rather than assuming it is automatically safe.
