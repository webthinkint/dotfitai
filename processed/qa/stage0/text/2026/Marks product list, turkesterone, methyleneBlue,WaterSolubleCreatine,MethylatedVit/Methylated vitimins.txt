Vitamin B6 does not typically undergo methylation to become active, unlike folate and B12. Instead, it is converted into its active coenzyme form, pyridoxal-5-phosphate (P5P or PLP). However, B6 is a critical cofactor that supports the methylation cycle and is necessary for producing the methyl donor SAMe. 
Key Details on Vitamin B6 and Methylation:
Active Form: While not "methylated" (e.g., methyl-cobalamin), the active, bioavailable form of Vitamin B6 is pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (P5P), often found in high-quality methylated B-complex supplements.
Role in Methylation: Vitamin B6 (as P5P) is essential for the conversion of homocysteine into cysteine via the cystathionine beta-synthase (CBS) enzyme.
Supports Methylation Cycle: It is required for the proper functioning of the entire one-carbon metabolism (methylation) pathway.

Great question — and this is highly relevant if you're formulating a practitioner-grade MVM where form matters.
Your pharmacist is partially correct in the strict chemical sense, but incomplete in the functional sense.
If we define “methylated” literally (meaning the molecule already contains a methyl group –CH₃ attached as part of its active structure), then only a few vitamins are commercially available in true methylated forms.
Let’s break this down precisely.

✅ Vitamins That Exist in TRUE Methylated Forms
1️⃣ Vitamin B9 – Folate
5Methyltetrahydrofolate
Also called L-5-MTHF
Branded forms: Quatrefolic®, Metafolin®
This is the biologically active, methyl-donor form
Bypasses MTHFR conversion
Highly stable for use in multivitamins
✔ Fully methylated
✔ Very common in high-quality MVMs

2️⃣ Vitamin B12
Methylcobalamin
One of the active coenzyme forms of B12
Direct methyl donor
Used in methylation cycle (methionine synthase reaction)
Other B12 forms:
Adenosylcobalamin (mitochondrial form)
Hydroxocobalamin
Cyanocobalamin
✔ Methylcobalamin is truly methylated
✔ Stable in properly formulated MVMs

⚠️ Vitamins That Are NOT “Methylated” (But Often Confused As Such)
These do NOT exist as methylated vitamins:
Vitamin B6 (exists as P5P – pyridoxal-5-phosphate, not methylated)
Riboflavin (B2) – exists as riboflavin-5-phosphate, not methylated
Thiamin (B1) – no methyl form
Niacin (B3) – no methyl form
Pantothenic acid (B5) – no methyl form
Biotin – no methyl form
Vitamins A, D, E, K – no methylated forms
There are activated or coenzyme forms, but not methylated versions.

🧬 Why Only B9 and B12 Can Be Methylated
Because they directly participate in one-carbon metabolism and methyl group transfer reactions:
5-MTHF donates methyl groups
Methylcobalamin transfers methyl groups to homocysteine
No other vitamin functions primarily as a methyl donor in this way.
