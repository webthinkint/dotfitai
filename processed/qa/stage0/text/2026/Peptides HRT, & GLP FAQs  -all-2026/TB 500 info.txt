TB-500
TB-500 is a synthetic fragment of Thymosin Beta-4 (TB4), a naturally occurring protein found throughout the body. It promotes cellular migration and tissue remodeling. Because TB-500 effects appear systemic rather than localized, unlike BPC-157, injection location is generally not injury site specific to determine where healing occur – i.e., TB-500 is typically used as a whole-body recovery peptide rather than a site-specific peptide. If using it for acute injury support, then the duration is generally 4–8 weeks. For significant Tendon/Ligament Injuries, 8–12 weeks may be appropriate, with a tapering in the final 2-4 weeks.
Goal/primary use: support tissue repair, recovery, and reduced inflammation—commonly used for healing injuries or improving mobility including:
• Soft tissue injury support; tendon and ligament recovery; muscle strain recovery
• Mobility and flexibility support; exercise recovery enhancement; General tissue healing
Mechanism*: enhances actin polymerization, increases angiogenesis, and promotes cell migration, accelerating wound repair and reducing fibrosis.
Dose
First 8 weeks: 2.5 mg twice weekly
Week 9-12: 2.5 mg once weekly
Week 13 off unless maintenance is warranted 
2–5 mg every few weeks is often used as a maintenance
Injection site: abdomen is typically the easiest and most commonly used but thigh, buttocks areas also used -practitioner preference since efficacy is not site specific.
Notes
Always complete nutrition programming (including longevity supporting supplementation), sleep, load management, and rehab first – all which you have done
TP-500 is not FDA-approved for human therapeutic use but commonly used safely
Human data are limited; most mechanistic evidence comes from animal models, but empirical (practical human experience) is strong favoring safe results
Because peptides mimic natural substances, when they are metabolized in the body, their constituent AAs can be recycled with little to no stress on organs or metabolism unlike most drugs.
*Purported mechanisms: Increases cellular migration to damaged tissue; promotes angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation); supporting tissue remodeling and repair; regulating inflammation during healing; enhancing actin regulation within cells, which is important for cell movement and repair processes.

For subcutaneous administration, practitioners typically use:

Upper thigh

These sites are chosen primarily for convenience and comfort, not because they improve efficacy.

What About Injecting Near the Injury?

For TB-500:

There is little evidence that injecting near an injured tendon, ligament, or muscle provides additional benefit.
Most peptide clinics and practitioners simply administer it subcutaneously in the abdomen.
The injection location is generally viewed as irrelevant to where the peptide ultimately acts.

For BPC-157:

Many practitioners do inject near the injury site.
This practice is based largely on anecdotal experience and theoretical mechanisms rather than strong human clinical data.
