MOTS-c is a peptide that works by moving between mitochondria and the nucleus to regulate cellular metabolism. It's triggered by stress and works similarly to a hormone to improve insulin sensitivity, promote glucose uptake, and enhance muscle function. It achieves this primarily by activating the AMPK pathway, which is a key regulator of energy balance in the cell. 
How MOTS-c works in the cell

Mitochondrial origin: 

MOTS-c is encoded by a gene in the mitochondrial DNA. 

Stress-induced release: 

Under metabolic stress, it moves from the mitochondria to the cytoplasm and then can translocate to the nucleus. 
AMPK activation: 
In the cytoplasm, MOTS-c activates the AMPK pathway, which regulates the cell's energy balance. 

Nuclear regulation: 

Once in the nucleus, it regulates gene expression to promote adaptive responses to stress and metabolic dysfunction. 

Exercise-mimicking effects: 

MOTS-c levels increase with exercise and mimick its benefits by improving muscle function, endurance, and energy production. 
How MOTS-c works in the body

Improves insulin sensitivity: 

It helps muscles take up glucose more effectively, which combats insulin resistance. 

Enhances glucose and fat metabolism: 

It influences how the cell uses glucose and fats for energy, promoting energy production and utilization. 

Modulates inflammation: 

It helps regulate inflammation, which is a key factor in aging and many chronic diseases. 

Supports bone health: 

It promotes bone formation by stimulating osteoblasts and inhibits bone breakdown by affecting osteoclasts. 

Protects against obesity: 

It has been shown to reduce weight gain and fat accumulation in models of diet-induced and age-related obesity
