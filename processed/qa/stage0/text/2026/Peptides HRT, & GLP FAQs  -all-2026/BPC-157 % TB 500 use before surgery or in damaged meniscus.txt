would using BPC-157 and TB 500 do any good for a torn meniscus if you have not had the surgery yet
Possibly, but with an important caveat: BPC-157 and TB-500 are unlikely to "heal" a torn meniscus, especially if the tear is large, displaced, or located in the poorly vascularized inner portion of the meniscus.
The rationale for using them before surgery is that they may help:
Reduce pain and inflammation 
Improve healing of surrounding soft tissues (capsule, ligaments, tendon attachments) 
Potentially enhance healing in the outer ("red zone") portion of the meniscus where blood supply exists 
Improve function and allow better participation in physical therapy 
Potentially reduce the need for surgery in some minor tears 
However, the challenge is that the meniscus has limited blood supply, particularly in the inner two-thirds. A mechanical tear that causes:
Locking 
Catching 
Joint instability 
A displaced flap or bucket-handle tear 
typically will not be corrected by peptides because the tissue remains mechanically torn regardless of inflammation status.
If I were looking at this clinically
Small degenerative tear or minor traumatic tear:
Physical therapy 
Strengthening around the knee 
Weight management if needed 
Activity modification 
Possible BPC-157/TB-500 trial 
In this situation, some individuals report meaningful improvement and may avoid surgery.
Large tear with mechanical symptoms:
Peptides may reduce symptoms and improve recovery capacity. 
They are unlikely to replace a necessary surgical repair or meniscectomy. 
Why some surgeons don't object
Even if surgery is ultimately required, entering surgery with:
Less inflammation 
Better quadriceps strength 
Better range of motion 
Less pain 
often leads to a better postoperative outcome. Therefore, using BPC-157/TB-500 during the prehabilitation phase is not an unreasonable strategy, although evidence in humans remains limited.
What the evidence actually says
The strongest support for BPC-157 and TB-500 comes from:
Animal studies 
Mechanistic data 
Anecdotal clinical reports 
High-quality human trials specifically showing healing of torn menisci are essentially lacking. So any recommendation is based more on biological plausibility and clinical experience than definitive evidence.
Practical takeaway
For a torn meniscus that has not yet undergone surgery, BPC-157 and TB-500 may help reduce pain, inflammation, and improve the healing environment around the knee, particularly when combined with a good rehabilitation program. They are most likely to help with symptom improvement and recovery capacity, but they should not be expected to reliably repair a significant meniscal tear that would otherwise require surgical intervention.
