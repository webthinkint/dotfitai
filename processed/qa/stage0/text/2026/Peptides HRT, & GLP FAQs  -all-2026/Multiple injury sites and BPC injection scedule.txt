I looked specifically for evidence comparing once-daily versus twice-daily BPC-157, including the newer literature. The answer is fairly straightforward:
There is currently no human evidence showing that twice-daily BPC-157 is superior to once-daily administration for healing an injury—or multiple injuries. A 2026 review notes that human subcutaneous pharmacokinetics have not even been characterized, so there isn't a scientifically established SC dosing interval. 
For your example of both shoulders plus a knee, there's therefore no evidence-based reason to think you need three injections, or that dividing a prescribed daily amount into two injections would make it work better at three different tissues. The animal literature suggests systemic biological effects across tendon, ligament, muscle, and bone injury models, but translating that into a human dosing schedule is still speculative. 
One interesting wrinkle is that the extremely limited human IV pharmacokinetic work suggests circulating BPC-157 itself has a short half-life (<30 minutes). That might sound like an argument for twice-daily dosing, but it really isn't: biological effects can substantially outlast measurable plasma concentrations, and we don't know the SC absorption curve in humans. Thus, there's no evidence that keeping blood BPC-157 concentrations elevated longer improves healing. 
So I wouldn't escalate from once to twice daily solely because there are three areas bothering you. If a clinician has prescribed a particular daily dose, I'd stick with that schedule rather than increasing injection frequency or total exposure based on the number of injuries.
There's also an important update since we've discussed BPC-157 previously: FDA held a Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee meeting in July 2026 specifically addressing BPC-157. FDA continues to emphasize that human safety information is inadequate and raises concerns about immunogenicity, peptide impurities, and API characterization. 
In practical terms, I'd separate the two questions:
Injection location: no convincing evidence that shoulder/knee-targeted SC injections beat ordinary abdominal SC administration.
Frequency: no convincing evidence that twice daily beats once daily, or that multiple injuries require more frequent administration.
That makes once-daily systemic administration the simpler approach rather than trying to chase three injuries with multiple injections, assuming BPC-157 is being used under a clinician's supervision in the first place. The much bigger unknown is whether BPC-157 meaningfully accelerates human musculoskeletal healing at all; the human evidence remains very limited. 
Given that you mentioned the injuries are minor, rehab/loading management is considerably better supported by human evidence than manipulating BPC-157 dose frequency or injection location.
