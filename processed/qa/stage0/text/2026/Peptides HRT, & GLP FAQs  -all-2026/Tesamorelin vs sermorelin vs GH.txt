| Primary action | Increases endogenous GH pulses → raises IGF-1 | Increases endogenous GH pulses → raises IGF-1 (usually less robust) | Raises GH/IGF-1 directly; more continuous exposure depending on use |
| FDA status | Approved (for HIV-associated lipodystrophy) | Not FDA-approved | Approved for specific medical indications (e.g., GH deficiency), not for physique/performance |
| Typical dosing pattern (high-level) | Commonly once daily | Often once or twice daily | Often daily (varies widely under medical care) |
| Typical “duration of use” | Often months (e.g., 12–26+ weeks) | Often weeks to months | Often months to years in true deficiency; off-label patterns vary |
| Strongest evidence-backed outcome | Visceral fat reduction (best-known human data) | GH-axis support claims are common; human outcomes are less consistent | Lean mass / body comp changes can be strong, but risk profile higher |
| Effect on natural GH production | Generally preserves pituitary function (stimulates it) | Generally preserves pituitary function (stimulates it) | Can suppress endogenous GH via feedback while on therapy |
| Physiologic “pulsatility” | More physiologic than GH (still a medication) | Often most “pulse-like” but shorter/less sustained | Often least physiologic (more exposure outside normal pulses) |
| Commonly reported pros | Systemic, consistent; evidence in visceral fat | Short-acting; “gentler” feel for some users | Potent, predictable elevations; clinically useful in deficiency |
| Commonly reported drawbacks | Raises IGF-1; side effects possible (fluid retention, paresthesia) | Less predictable response; may require more frequent dosing | Higher side-effect burden; more oversight needed |

Quick “when clients ask” summary (1–2 lines each)
Tesamorelin: “A prescription peptide that stimulates your pituitary to release GH—best known for visceral fat reduction over months.”
Sermorelin: “A shorter-acting GHRH peptide that also stimulates GH release, but results can be less consistent and protocols vary.”
GH: “Direct hormone therapy—can be potent but carries the most risk and requires strict medical indication and monitoring.”
