Yes, there have been human clinical trials involving GHK-Cu, but they are primarily topical studies in dermatology and wound healing rather than injectable peptide trials like those for tesamorelin or GLP-1 agonists. The evidence supporting injectable GHK-Cu for systemic anti-aging, muscle recovery, or orthopedic injuries is currently much weaker and largely based on animal studies, laboratory research, and anecdotal clinical use.

Mechanism of action
GHK-Cu (glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine-copper) is a naturally occurring copper-binding tripeptide found in human plasma that declines with age. It appears to:
Stimulate fibroblasts 
Increase collagen I and III synthesis 
Increase elastin and glycosaminoglycan production 
Improve angiogenesis (new blood vessel formation) 
Reduce inflammatory cytokines 
Modulate matrix metalloproteinases involved in tissue remodeling 
Influence expression of thousands of genes related to tissue repair and inflammation 
Unlike BPC-157 or TB-500, GHK-Cu's strongest evidence is extracellular matrix remodeling, making it particularly attractive for skin and connective tissue repair. 
Human clinical trials
Published human studies have shown improvements in:
Fine wrinkles 
Skin elasticity 
Skin density 
Photodamaged skin 
Overall skin appearance 
Most studies lasted 8–24 weeks using topical creams or serums. Improvements were generally modest but statistically significant and comparable to or better than some established cosmetic ingredients, with excellent tolerability. 
A randomized clinical trial is also underway evaluating topical GHK-Cu gel for acute wound healing, reflecting the growing interest in its regenerative potential. 
Injectable dosing
Because there are no standardized FDA-approved dosing protocols, injectable dosing is based on clinical practice rather than definitive trials.
The most commonly reported protocols are:
1-2 mg subcutaneously once daily 
Duration: 4-8 weeks 
Some clinicians extend treatment to 8-12 weeks for connective tissue repair. 
Many regenerative medicine clinics also use:
2 mg/day for 6-8 weeks 
Followed by a maintenance break before another cycle. 
These protocols are empirical and have not been validated in large randomized controlled trials.
Topical dosing
Most cosmetic products contain approximately:
0.05-0.2% GHK-Cu 
Applied once or twice daily 
This is where the strongest human evidence currently exists.
Safety
Overall, GHK-Cu appears to have an excellent safety profile.
Reported issues are generally limited to:
Mild injection-site irritation (injectable use) 
Temporary skin irritation (topical use) 
Rare allergic reactions 
One theoretical concern is that because GHK-Cu promotes tissue growth and remodeling, many clinicians recommend avoiding it in individuals with active malignancy until more safety data are available. This precaution is based on theory rather than evidence of harm. 
Practical takeaway
If you compare the regenerative peptides discussed so far:
BPC-157 appears strongest for tendon, ligament, gastrointestinal, and soft tissue repair (though still lacking robust human trials). 
TB-500 is primarily aimed at cell migration, angiogenesis, and broad tissue healing, with human evidence remaining sparse. 
GHK-Cu has the best human evidence of the three for skin regeneration and collagen remodeling, but far less evidence for injectable systemic use than is often claimed online. 
For someone interested in improving skin quality, wound healing, scar remodeling, or connective tissue health, GHK-Cu is one of the more scientifically plausible peptides available. However, claims regarding whole-body rejuvenation, muscle growth, longevity, or dramatic orthopedic healing remain speculative until larger, well-controlled human studies are completed.
