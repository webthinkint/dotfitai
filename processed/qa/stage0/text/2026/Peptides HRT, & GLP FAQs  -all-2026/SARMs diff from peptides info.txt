SARMs and peptides differ fundamentally in their chemical composition, mechanism of action, and regulatory status. Peptides are short chains of amino acids that act as natural signaling molecules, while SARMs are synthetic, non-steroidal drugs that mimic testosterone by directly binding to androgen receptors in specific tissues. 
Key Differences
| Feature | SARMs (Selective Androgen Receptor Modulators) | Peptides |
| Composition | Synthetic, non-steroidal compounds | Naturally occurring short chains of amino acids (small proteins) |
| Mechanism | Directly bind to androgen receptors in muscle and bone to signal growth, mimicking testosterone | Act as signaling molecules, stimulating natural bodily processes like growth hormone production |
| Primary Use (Bodybuilding) | Faster muscle growth and fat loss with potentially fewer side effects than traditional steroids | Enhance natural recovery, growth, skin health, and cognitive function |
| Regulation | Investigational drugs, not approved by the FDA for any medical use, and often associated with health risks when misused | Some are FDA-approved for specific conditions; many are used under medical supervision in therapy |
| Side Effects | Potential risks include liver injury, hormone suppression, and cardiovascular issues | Generally considered safer with potential mild side effects like injection site irritation, headaches, or water retention |
Summary
SARMs offer a targeted, synthetic approach to building muscle mass, similar to steroids but with a higher degree of tissue selectivity, though they carry significant health warnings from regulatory bodies like the FDA.
Peptides offer a more "natural" approach by encouraging the body to produce its own beneficial hormones and compounds, resulting in a broader range of benefits beyond just muscle gain, such as improved recovery, sleep, and skin health. 
It is important to note that both compounds should be approached with caution and medical oversight, as their long-term effects and purity are often inconsistent when sourced from unregulated suppliers.
