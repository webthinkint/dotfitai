What is an Apo B blood test?
An Apo B or Apolipoprotein B-100 test is a blood test that can tell you about your risk for cardiovascular (heart and blood vessel) disease. To do this, it measures the amount of Apo B, which carries substances in your blood that help make plaque, a waxy fat that can block your arteries.
Apolipoprotein B takes lipids where they’re going. But it only gives rides to the kind of lipids that have a bad reputation: low-density lipoprotein (LDL), very low-density lipoprotein (VLDL) and others. You won’t find the good cholesterol, high-density lipoproteins (HDL), hanging around with Apo B. When you see Apo B, you can be sure LDL is there, too.
Some healthcare providers believe an Apo B test is more accurate than a lipid panel (which measures all those cholesterol types) to estimate cardiovascular disease risk.

Optimal ApoB Targets by Risk Category
Medical organizations recommend the following targets:
Optimal / Low-Risk: <60 - 80 mg/dL
Moderate Risk: <90 mg/dL
High Risk (e.g., existing heart disease or diabetes): <80 mg/dL
Very High Risk (e.g., recurrent cardiovascular events): <65 mg/dL

Average Ranges by Age and Sex
While "average" isn't necessarily "optimal" for heart health, comparing your score to population percentiles can put your numbers into perspective. Typical population ranges (in mg/dL) include: [1]
Age < 40: 50 - 150 mg/dL
Age 40 - 60: 60 - 160 mg/dL
Age > 60: 70 - 170 mg/dL [1, 2, 3, 4]
Note: The general population average often sits around 100 mg/dL, but modern preventive cardiology considers this too high for ideal, long-term cardiovascular health. [1, 2]
What type of results do you get and what do the results mean?
Your Apolipoprotein B -100 test results can range from 20 to 400 milligrams per deciliter (mg/dL).
If your Apo B is higher than 130 mg/dL, you’re at a higher risk of heart and blood vessel disease.
Some cardiology guidelines recommend a target of less than 65 or 80 mg/dL of Apo B. They suggest these goals for people between ages 40 and 75 who take statins.
What is a normal Apo B level?
A normal Apo B level is:
66 to 133 mg/dL for males
60 to 117 mg/dL for females
Conditions that can make your Apo B test results higher
Your Apo B test results may be higher if you have:
A pregnancy.
High cholesterol.
Nephrotic syndrome, a kidney issue.


Apo B testing
An ApoB (Apolipoprotein B-100) blood test measures the number of particles in your blood that carry "bad" cholesterol (LDL). It is considered a more accurate marker for cardiovascular disease risk than a standard LDL-C test, as it directly counts atherogenic particles. High levels indicate increased risk for plaque buildup and cardiovascular disease, often used to monitor treatment. 
Key Aspects of the ApoB Test:
Purpose: Measures the main protein found in LDL and other fat-carrying particles.
Significance: Because each LDL particle contains one ApoB molecule, this test directly measures the total count of dangerous particles, better identifying risk when LDL cholesterol seems normal
When it is used: Recommended for people with diabetes, obesity, high triglycerides, or a family history of heart disease, as well as for monitoring the effectiveness of statin therapy.
