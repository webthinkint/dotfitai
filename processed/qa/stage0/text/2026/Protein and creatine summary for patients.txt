Key Points
Protein = building blocks; creatine = quick energy for muscles
Safe for healthy people; strong research backing
Typical intakes: protein 1.2–1.6 g/kg/day, creatine 3–5 g/day
Best for people lifting weights, older adults, low appetite
Exercise does most of the work; supplements are “extras”
This article explains what protein powders and creatine do, how much people usually take, who they’re most useful for, and why exercise still matters more than any supplement. What protein and creatine do Protein powder (whey or plant-based) is basically a convenient food: it helps you reach your daily protein target to support muscle repair, growth, and healthy aging, especially if you can’t get enough from meals.1 Creatine is an energy helper stored in muscles; it fuels short, hard efforts like sprinting or heavy lifting and can improve strength, power, training capacity, and muscle size.1 Are they safe and effective? Higher-protein diets have not been shown to damage healthy kidneys, despite old myths.1 Creatine monohydrate is one of the most studied sports supplements and has a very favorable safety and benefit profile in healthy people.1 Typical amounts and best types - Protein: For most adults who care about muscle or healthy aging, a common target is 1.2–1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight per day (more for older adults, people losing weight, or heavy lifters).1 - Creatine: 3–5 grams per day is enough for almost everyone.1 Good choices are whey isolate for omnivores and plant-based blends with added leucine for vegans; for creatine, stick with plain creatine monohydrate from a reputable brand.1 Who may benefit most and key message People who tend to benefit most include those doing resistance training, older adults, people on appetite-suppressing weightloss medications, and anyone who struggles to eat enough protein.1 The core takeaway: strength training builds the “cake,” protein is a thin layer of icing, and creatine is just the sprinkles—exercise is the main driver of results; supplements just give a modest boost.1 Would you like a one-page handout you can give patients summarizing how to safely use protein powder and creatine?
