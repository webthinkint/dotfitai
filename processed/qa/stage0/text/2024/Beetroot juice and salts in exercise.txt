Beetroot Review  

Beetroot juice (BRJ) and nitrate salts (NIT) are compared in terms of their effects on exercise performance and physiological function.
​
BRJ may have additional advantages compared to NIT, such as reducing oxygen consumption during high-intensity exercise, enhancing performance improvements with training, and improving recovery post-exercise.
​
The potential benefits of BRJ beyond nitrate may be due to its rich spectrum of phytonutrients, including polyphenols, which could have antioxidant and/or anti-inflammatory properties.
​
Studies comparing BRJ and NIT have shown mixed results, with some studies indicating greater benefits with BRJ and others showing no significant differences.
​
Further research is needed to directly compare the effects of BRJ and NIT on exercise-related outcomes, taking into account factors such as the amount of nitrate in the supplements, blinding of participants, and the use of reproducible physiological outcomes.
​
Dietary nitrate supplementation, including beetroot juice, has various beneficial effects on exercise performance and physiological adaptations.
​
Nitrate from beetroot juice is converted to nitrite and then to nitric oxide in the body, which plays a role in vasodilation and improving blood flow.
​
The effects of dietary nitrate supplementation can vary depending on factors such as dosage, duration of supplementation, and individual response.
Other nitrate-rich vegetables and fruits can also provide similar benefits to beetroot juice.
