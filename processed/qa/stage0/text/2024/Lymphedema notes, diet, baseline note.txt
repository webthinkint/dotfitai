Lymphedema is a chronic condition that causes swelling in the body's soft tissues due to a buildup of lymph fluid. Lymph fluid is a protein-rich fluid that's usually drained through the lymphatic system, but when lymph vessels or nodes become damaged or blocked, the flow of lymph is affected. Lymphedema most commonly affects the arms or legs, but it can also occur in the chest wall, abdomen, neck, and genitals. 
Symptoms of lymphedema include:
Swelling in an arm or leg
Pain or discomfort
Difficulty wearing jewelry or watches or fitting into clothes or shoes
A heavy or tight feeling in the arms or legs
Restricted range of motion in the limb
Tingling sensation in the affected limb
Recurring skin infections
Thickening and hardening of the skin
Blisters or wart-like growths on the skin
Severe fatigue 
There is no known cure for lymphedema, but a medical professional can manage the condition to improve symptoms. Treatments include exercise, wrapping, massage, and compression. In severe cases, infections can place greater stress on the lymphatic system and may require hospital admission.
