The quick answer is that the 1000s of studies that proves creatine delivers on claims of safety and efficacy all use creatine monohydrate without a delivery system such as liposomes*. Everyone is trying to find a different complex that might work as well as just the creatine monohydrate, so that less creatine is needed to get the same (or better) results, thus gain a marketing advantage. It has not happened yet (see below from the ISSN), as there are no human studies showing an advantage with a liposome creatine. The good news is that creatine liposomal (or liposomal creatine) is still creatine monohydrate, so it doesn’t hurt. But the question is, does the liposome addition (fat delivery system) allow a better or lesser result. The only way to know would be to perform at least 50 different clinical trials vs creatine monohydrate alone, and let the winner prevail. However, there is probably no point, since we know creatine monohydrate works optimally at a determined dosage by body weight, and more doesn’t alter the outcome, so how does getting more to the target tissue matter? That said, it would be great if instead of using .045gms/lb of body weight of creatine monohydrate as a maintenance dose to maximizes skeletal muscle (and brain) stores, you could accomplish the same result with less, thus potentially saving money, if the theoretical formula is less expensive.  
And yes, you should always take your creatine supplementation with food or shake to maximize skeletal muscle uptake.
*Liposomal refers to a drug preparation that uses tiny, fat-like particles to deliver an active drug to the body.
