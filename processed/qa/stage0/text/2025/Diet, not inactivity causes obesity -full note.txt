Diet, not inactivity, drives obesity in developed nations 
A groundbreaking study published in PNAS reveals that people in developed nations burn a similar amount of calories daily as those in less-developed nations, challenging the notion that inactivity is the primary cause of obesity. Led by Herman Pontzer, the research analyzed data from over 4,000 individuals across 34 countries, finding that increased energy intake, particularly from ultraprocessed foods, is a more significant factor in the obesity epidemic. "This study confirms what I've been saying, which is that diet is the key culprit in our current [obesity] epidemic," said Barry Popkin, a professor at the Gillings School of Global Public Health at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.
