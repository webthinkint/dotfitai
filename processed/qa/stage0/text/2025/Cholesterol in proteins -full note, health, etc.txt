More from the latest peer review research:
In healthy humans who are not “cholesterol sensitive”, consuming moderate amounts of dietary cholesterol—from foods like eggs, shellfish, or dairy—does not significantly raise blood cholesterol levels or increase cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk. Here’s a breakdown of the evidence:

1. Dietary vs. Blood Cholesterol
The body tightly regulates blood cholesterol through endogenous synthesis (mainly in the liver).
When dietary cholesterol intake increases, the liver typically reduces internal cholesterol production to maintain balance.
Therefore, for most people, dietary cholesterol has minimal impact on blood LDL (“bad”) or HDL (“good”) cholesterol.

2. The “Cholesterol-Responder” Minority
About 15–25% of individuals are “hyper-responders.”
These people experience a noticeable rise in LDL and HDL cholesterol when they consume more dietary cholesterol.
However, even in hyper-responders, the LDL:HDL ratio—a key predictor of heart risk—often remains unchanged or only slightly affected.

3. Eggs and Dairy Evidence
Eggs: Large meta-analyses (e.g., BMJ 2013, AJCN 2020) show that eating up to one egg per day is not associated with increased heart disease or stroke in healthy individuals.
Dairy: Full-fat dairy foods (like cheese or yogurt) contain cholesterol and saturated fat, yet studies show neutral or even beneficial effects on lipid profiles, possibly due to calcium, probiotics, and bioactive peptides.
Mechanism: The overall diet pattern (fiber, fat quality, caloric balance) has a much greater influence on blood lipids than cholesterol from whole foods.

4. Modern Dietary Guidelines
Most expert groups, including the American Heart Association and Dietary Guidelines for Americans (2020–2025), no longer set a strict cholesterol limit (formerly 300 mg/day).
Instead, the focus is on overall diet quality—especially limiting saturated and trans fats, maintaining healthy weight, and consuming plenty of plants and unsaturated fats.

✅ Summary Takeaway:
For healthy people without familial hypercholesterolemia or metabolic dysregulation, moderate consumption of foods naturally containing cholesterol—like eggs, shrimp, or dairy—does not raise cholesterol to harmful levels and can fit easily within a heart-healthy diet.
