Q (part 2): Why do amino acid formulas have no calories posted for the amount of amino acids?
A: In the United States, individual amino acid formulas (such as in AminoFormula and BCAAs) don’t count as protein because they are not a complete protein, thus the manufactures can’t publish calories. At this time the US believes it would be confusing and the amount trivial. In Europe, the manufacturers must claim the calories for the aminos in most products regardless, and the US may soon follow suit (see below from experts). At the end of the day, one gram of amino acids has approximately 4 calories, but it doesn’t necessarily act like a complete protein in the body, making it difficult to quantify and fairly trivial in the big picture of daily calorie intake – i.e., most people would only use very small amounts of amino acids outside their complete food/protein intake. 
The calories listed on AX are from are from the carbs (those calories come from the flavors). 
From industry experts:
“There’s no way to put these calories on a Supplement Facts panel in a way that people would recognize as a source of calories.” You’re presenting the consumer with an inherent contradiction. By presenting calories but not a source (i.e., carbohydrate, protein, or fat) then a consumer could argue this is also misleading making companies stuck between a rock and a hard place.”

Q (part 1): There is no protein listed on AminoFormula, can I count it as a protein?
A: The reason AminoXXXL (AX) does not publish a protein amount is because it is purposely not a complete protein as it contains only the 8-amino acids that are directly responsible for protein synthesis activation and therefore serves as a low calorie, fast acting, high anabolic signaling product for activating muscle protein synthesis (MPS) beyond intact protein itself. Your total food/shake protein intake throughout the day supplies the precursors and remaining amino acids necessary for the complete and prolonged synthesis.
More info:
AX serves to enhance the MPS process beyond protein alone or serve as a low calorie, high anabolic extra source of protein. A protein equivalent in 1-scoop of AX, can be ~25gms as it takes ~40gms of a complete protein to get the amount of the 8 EAA found in one serving of AX including the 6gms of BCAA. You could add the 25gm number once a day to your protein intake as part of your 1gm/LB/lean body mass (LBM)/day of protein needs - although active strength/performance athletes still maintain the 1gm/LB/LBM/D of whole protein sources because whole protein has other helpful properties.
