## Slide 261

Part III Advanced Therapies Summary from The Road to Health and Wellness, Especially Men 40+

Summary of Final Phase
1. Foundation First: Metabolic Reset
Before considering any “anti-aging” therapy, spend at least 6 months optimizing nutrition, fitness, and micronutrient intake.
A well-functioning metabolism makes most trendy “anti-aging” ingredients unnecessary and ensures any later therapy works more effectively.
2. Evaluating Purported Anti-Aging Ingredients
Popular compounds such as NMN, NAD⁺, HMB, resveratrol, urolithin A, ergothioneine, methylene blue, spirulina/greens often show a “thread of truth” but lack robust evidence in well-nourished, metabolically healthy adults.
Trainers should help clients avoid the “Internet distraction” of unqualified advice.
3. When to Consider Advanced Interventions
Only after lifestyle and baseline nutrition fail to achieve health or performance goals comparable to peers, and symptoms or lab data justify it (e.g., fatigue, sleep disturbance, low libido, chronic pain, unmanageable weight gain).
Decisions must weigh risk/benefit ratios and be grounded in current clinical evidence

4. Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT)
Testosterone therapy: Indicated when fasted morning T is <250–300 ng/dL on two separate tests and symptoms are present.
Restoration in testosterone-deficient men is generally cardiovascularly safe and may provide metabolic/longevity benefits, but therapy is not recommended for men with normal T.
Therapy is medical—not cosmetic—and should always be supervised by a physician.
5. Peptide Therapies
Peptides are short amino-acid chains that signal hormone release (e.g., Sermorelin, Ipamorelin, CJC-1295, BPC-157, IGF-1 LR3, MK-677).
These “grey-area” peptides are experimental, not FDA-approved for anti-aging, and banned in competitive sport (WADA).
Evidence is mostly extrapolated from GH/IGF-1 research and primarily effective only in deficient subjects.
Oral peptides are poorly absorbed; injectables are required for potential effect.
6. Growth Hormone & IGF-1 Basics
GH acts as the “general contractor” initiating tissue growth; IGF-1 is the “construction crew” carrying it out.
IGF-1 blood levels (not one-time GH tests) best reflect average GH status.

7. GLP-1 Receptor Agonist (GLP-1RA) Drugs for Weight Loss
Drugs like semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) mimic gut hormones to suppress appetite and lower blood sugar.
Indicated for obesity or type 2 diabetes when diet/exercise fail.
Side effects: nausea, GI distress, rare pancreatitis, significant lean body mass loss (up to ~50% of weight lost), and micronutrient deficits.
8. Trainer’s Role with GLP-1 Users
Muscle-preserving resistance training, high-protein/micronutrient-dense daily nutrition, and monitoring nutrient gaps are essential to counteract lean mass loss and support safe drug discontinuation.
9. Overarching Guidance
Trainers should refer clients to medical professionals for diagnostics and therapy.
Their key value lies in establishing and maintaining the nutrition/exercise foundation that makes any advanced therapy safer and more effective

## Slide 264

Top 10 Trainer/Practitioner Takeaways from Part III The Road to Health and Wellness, Especially Men 40+
Metabolism First – Require a 6-month baseline nutrition and fitness program before exploring any anti-aging or weight-loss drug interventions.
Evidence over Hype – Evaluate the strength of clinical data and individual physiological context before recommending or supporting any therapy.
Risk/Benefit Assessment – Discuss potential side effects and long-term consequences; only proceed when medical data justify it.
Lab-Driven Decisions – Hormone therapy decisions must be based on repeated lab work plus clinical symptoms, not anecdote.
Understand Regulatory Status – Many peptides and GH secretagogues are not FDA-approved and banned in sports.
Coach, Don’t Prescribe – Provide education and referral, not medical prescriptions; trainers are facilitators, not prescribers.
Counter Muscle Loss in GLP-1 Users – Implement resistance training and targeted nutrition to protect lean body mass.
Close Nutrition Gaps – Recommend high-protein, micronutrient-dense “nutrition companion” meals to offset appetite suppression or drug-induced deficiencies.
Plan the Exit – Help clients transition off weight-loss drugs with sustainable habits to avoid rebound fat gain.
Career Differentiator – Position yourself as a trusted resource who can vet trends, cut through misinformation, and guide clients toward safe, evidence-based solutions.
