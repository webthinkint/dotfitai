More Information
Diindolylmethane (DIM), a compound commonly found in cruciferous vegetables  such as broccoli, brussels sprouts, cauliflower, etc. DIM supplements are not evidence based, meaning there is still no significant proof of their safety and effectiveness. A few clinical trials shows that DIM may support healthy estrogen levels. However, DIM supplements may be harmful to some women based on individual physiology. If you’re of reproductive age, estrogen is necessary to support healthy breast, bone, and endometrial tissue. If through menopause, DIM would have little to no benefit and in fact may actually lower your body’s ability to keep a healthy bone density. Supplements may also interfere with certain medications so always check with your doctor before use.
Dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) is a hormone that your body naturally produces in the adrenal gland. Therefore, it is the same basic answer for DHEA, since supplemental levels can act like a drug in the body and upset the natural hormonal feedback loop.
