Lipoprotein (a)
Their Lipoprotein (a) scoring is very deceptive. By using different measurements it scares someone not looking into conversions (most would never do it). They also made up their own reference interval that is not consistent with current science (i.e., <75nmol/L, which is 35mg/dl). It’s clear that the goal is to be <50mg/dl according to current science. “An assessment of five standard samples across a broad range of Lp(a) concentrations indicated that conversion to mg/dL can be approximated by dividing nmol/L values by 2.15 (r2 = 0.998 for linearity)”. Therefore, your score of 101.8nmol/L would equal to 47.3mg/dl, which is below the possible risk level of >50mg/dl or 107.5nmol/L. Moreover, even at >50mg/dl (>107.5nmol/L), you would have to have at least one more CVD risk factor(e.g., high cholesterol, triglycerides, blood pressure, etc.,)  for this to be a serious concern. You currently have no other risk factors.

Vitamin D Recommendations:
Note: 1.0 nmol/L = 0.4 ng/mL of 25-hydroxyvitamin D concentration (25(OH)D); 1 mcg D3 = 40 IUs

When using blood tests: anyone seeking or advised to increase their vitamin D status, as determined by their serum 25(OH)D concentration, to a level of 75-100 nmol/L (30-40 ng/mL) to maximize vitamin Ds contribution to the body’s structural development and tissue/organ maintenance throughout all phases of life. If below this standard level, start with one (1) soft gel daily and adjust as necessary.
Up to 70 years of age: serum 25(OH)D concentration of 75-100 nmol/L (30-40 ng/mL)
> 70 years old: serum 25(OH)D concentration of 100 nmol/L (~40 ng/mL)
Active adolescents and adult athletes: serum 25(OH)D concentration of 100-125 nmol/L (~40-50 ng/mL)
