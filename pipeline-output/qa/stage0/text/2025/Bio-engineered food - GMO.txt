What Are Bioengineered Ingredients?
Bioengineered (BE) ingredients are ingredients in food that come from plants, animals, or microorganisms whose DNA has been altered through modern biotechnology (genetic engineering).
In simple terms:
Bioengineered ingredients = ingredients made from genetically modified organisms (GMOs).
This definition is used legally in the U.S. National Bioengineered Food Disclosure Standard (NBFDS), which requires BE foods to be labeled.
✔️ Simple Definition for Consumers
Bioengineered ingredients are GMO-derived ingredients that still contain detectable genetically modified DNA or protein and therefore must be labeled under U.S. law.


📌 Key Points
1. BE Ingredients Contain Detectable Modified Genetic Material
To be labeled “bioengineered,” the ingredient must contain detectable GMO DNA or proteins.
If the GM DNA is removed or destroyed during processing (common with oils, sugars, starches), the product may NOT be labeled BE—even if the crop was genetically modified.

2. BE = GMO (legally, same category), but not all GMOs must be labeled
Examples of GM crops used in BE ingredients:
Corn
Soybeans
Canola
Sugar beets
Cottonseed
Papaya (Hawaiian)
Certain potatoes
Certain apples
Squash

Examples of Common Bioengineered Ingredients
These come from genetically modified crops and often remain BE after processing:
From Corn
High-fructose corn syrup
Corn starch (sometimes)
Corn flour (if not extensively processed)
From Soy
Soy protein concentrate/isolate
Soy lectithin (sometimes BE, sometimes not)
Soy oil (not labeled BE because the DNA is removed)
From Canola
Canola oil (usually not BE-labeled—DNA removed)
From Sugar Beets
Table sugar (often not BE-labeled because no DNA remains)
From Cottonseed
Cottonseed oil
From Potatoes/Apples
Certain varieties engineered to resist browning or bruising

What Foods Commonly Contain BE Ingredients?
Packaged snacks
Sweetened beverages
Breakfast cereals
Baking mixes
Protein bars
Frozen meals
Condiments
Many ultra-processed foods

What Foods Are Usually Free From Bioengineered Ingredients?
Organic products (by law, organic cannot contain GMOs)
Whole, fresh animal products (meat, eggs, dairy)
Whole fruits and vegetables (except a few GM varieties)

Why Are BE Ingredients Used?
Companies use bioengineered crops because they offer:
Higher yields
Pest resistance
Herbicide tolerance
Reduced food waste (e.g., non-browning apples)
Cost savings in large-scale food production

⚖️ Labeling Rules (U.S.)
Foods must disclose BE status if:
They contain detectable modified DNA
Their ingredients are on the USDA’s BE list
Allowed forms of disclosure:
The word “bioengineered”
A symbol
Electronic/digital link (QR code)
