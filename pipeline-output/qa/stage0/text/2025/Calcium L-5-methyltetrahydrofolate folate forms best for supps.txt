No, they are not exactly the same; Calcium L-5-methyltetrahydrofolate is a form of 5-methyltetrahydrofolate that is stabilized by being a calcium salt. 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF) is the biologically active form of folate (Vitamin B9) that the body can use directly, while Calcium L-5-methyltetrahydrofolate is the common, stable, and commercially available form of that active nutrient used in supplements.  
Here's a breakdown:
5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5-MTHF):
.
This is the naturally occurring, metabolically active form of folate found in the human circulation and in food. It's the form the body uses to convert homocysteine to methionine, among other vital functions. 
Calcium L-5-methyltetrahydrofolate:
.
This is a synthetic, stable salt form of the active 5-MTHF. Adding calcium makes the naturally occurring 5-MTHF more stable and suitable for use in dietary supplements and food fortification. 
In summary:
When you see "5-MTHF," "L-5-MTHF," or "Calcium L-5-methyltetrahydrofolate" on a supplement label, it refers to the same biologically active nutrient. The "calcium" part simply indicates the form it's formulated in for stability and availability in a supplement.
