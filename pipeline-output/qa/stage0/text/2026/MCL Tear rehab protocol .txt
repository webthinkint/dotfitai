Yes. For an isolated Grade 2 MCL tear (partial tear) with a stable knee and no associated ACL/meniscus injury, nonoperative treatment is standard, and the prognosis is generally excellent. The emphasis is early protected motion, progressive loading, restoring quadriceps/hamstring/hip strength, then balance and sport-specific function—not prolonged immobilization. 
A practical protocol would look something like this:
| Phase | Approx. timing | Main goals & activities |
| 1. Protect & calm it down | Days 0–7 | Hinged MCL brace if recommended; weight-bear as tolerated, crutches if limping; ice/compression/elevation; avoid valgus stress, twisting and pivoting. Begin gentle heel slides, quad sets, straight-leg raises and comfortable knee ROM. |
| 2. Restore motion & basic strength | Weeks 1–3 | Progress toward normal walking and full extension/flexion. Stationary bike with little/no resistance. Quad sets, SLRs, hamstring curls, calf raises, bridges, hip abduction/adduction as tolerated, partial squats and light leg press. Start basic single-leg balance. |
| 3. Rebuild strength & stability | Weeks 3–6 | Progress squats, leg press, step-ups/step-downs, split squats/lunges, hamstring work, calf raises and hip/glute strengthening. Increase cycling/elliptical. Progress single-leg balance and proprioception. Still be cautious with aggressive lateral/cutting movements. |
| 4. Return to athletic movement | ~Weeks 4–8+ | Once walking, stairs and strength work are pain-free with no swelling: progress jogging → running → lateral movement → agility → plyometrics → sport-specific drills. Grade 2 athletes may undergo functional testing around 6–8 weeks. |
What I would emphasize
During the first 1–2 weeks, I wouldn't try to "test" the MCL repeatedly with side-to-side movements. The ligament needs some protection while it heals. A short hinged brace is commonly used, particularly when walking; Mass General's protocol describes bracing for roughly 3–6 weeks depending on severity, although individual sports-medicine practices vary considerably. 
At the same time, you generally don't want to shut the leg down completely. As pain and swelling permit, regain knee motion and maintain quad activation. Mass General includes quad sets, heel slides, straight-leg raises, short-arc extensions, hamstring curls, calf raises, hip abduction, partial squats and wall slides in its MCL program. 
A simple strength progression
Once ordinary walking is comfortable, I'd think of the progression as:
Bike → bilateral squat/leg press → step-ups → split squat → single-leg squat/step-down → dynamic balance → jogging → lateral movement → cutting/agility.
The important point is that progression should be criteria-based rather than simply calendar-based. Don't advance because "it's week 4"; advance because the knee has demonstrated that it can handle the previous workload.
For full return to sport, a good target is full ROM, essentially no pain, no swelling after exercise, no instability, and ≥90% quadriceps/hamstring/hip strength and functional performance compared with the other leg. Those are also essentially Sanford's return-to-sport criteria. 
One particularly important point for golf
If this is related to the golfer/MCL situation we've been discussing, golf is interesting because walking and straight-line strengthening can return relatively early, while the rotational forces of the golf swing should come later. I would separate "return to exercise" from "return to golf."
A sensible progression would be putting → chipping → short pitches → half swings → ¾ swings → full irons → driver → full round, provided there is no medial knee pain, swelling or sense of instability afterward. The exact progression also depends substantially on which knee is injured and whether the golfer swings right- or left-handed, because the loading and rotational demands are quite different.
For an uncomplicated Grade 2 MCL, 6–8 weeks to fairly normal athletic function is a reasonable general expectation, although some people progress faster and others take longer.

6-week protocol for golfer
Absolutely. Assuming this is an isolated Grade 2 MCL tear, the knee is stable enough for nonoperative treatment, and a sports-medicine clinician/PT has cleared progressive weight-bearing, here is a practical 6-week golf-oriented program. The calendar is a guide; pain, swelling and stability determine progression.
Week 1 — Protect, restore motion, maintain muscle
Goal: Reduce pain/swelling, protect the healing MCL, regain normal extension and begin restoring flexion.
Daily
Hinged knee brace for walking if prescribed. 
Weight-bearing as tolerated; crutches until walking without a significant limp. 
Ice/compression 15–20 min, particularly after exercise. 
Heel slides: 2 × 15 
Quad sets: 3 × 10, 5-sec holds 
Straight-leg raises: 3 × 10–15 
Side-lying hip abduction: 3 × 15 
Glute bridges: 3 × 12–15 
Standing calf raises: 3 × 15 
Gentle hamstring/calf stretching. 
Stationary bike 10–15 min, essentially no resistance, once ROM allows comfortable pedaling. 
Golf: Putting only if standing and putting are completely comfortable. No full swing or rotational loading.
Avoid: pivoting, twisting, lateral cutting, deep squatting and anything producing medial-knee pain.

Week 2 — Normalize walking + begin closed-chain strength
Goal: Normal gait, minimal swelling, near-full ROM and begin loading the leg.
Strength 3–4 days/week
Stationary bike: 15–25 min 
Bodyweight squat to ~45–60°: 3 × 12 
Light leg press, comfortable ROM: 3 × 12–15 
Step-ups, 4–6": 3 × 10 each leg 
Bridges: 3 × 15 
Hamstring curls: 3 × 12–15 
Calf raises: 3 × 15–20 
Hip abduction/band work: 3 × 15 
Single-leg balance: 3 × 30 sec 
Golf: Putting → short chipping if completely comfortable. Start with perhaps 20–30 balls, focusing on upper-body technique rather than aggressive lower-body rotation.
Progress when: normal walking, little/no swelling and exercises don't produce increased medial-knee pain the following day.

Week 3 — Build strength and single-leg control
This is where I would start treating the leg more like an athlete again.
Strength 3 days/week
Bike/elliptical: 20–30 min 
Squat: 3 × 10–12 
Leg press: 3 × 10–12 
Step-ups: 3 × 10 
Controlled step-downs: 3 × 8–10 
Split squat, shallow initially: 3 × 8–10 
Romanian deadlift: 3 × 10 
Hamstring curl: 3 × 10–12 
Calf raise: 3 × 15 
Hip/glute strengthening: 3 × 12–15 
Single-leg balance + reach: 3 rounds 
The major coaching point is excellent knee alignment—don't allow the knee to collapse inward and load the healing MCL.
Golf: Putting/chipping unrestricted if symptom-free. Add 30–50% swings with wedges and short irons. Start with ~20 balls and build toward 40–50.
Still no aggressive driver swings.

Week 4 — Strength + controlled rotation
Goal: Restore substantial leg strength and begin preparing the knee for rotational forces.
Strength 3 days/week
Squat: 3–4 × 8–10 
Leg press: 3–4 × 8–10 
Split squat: 3 × 8–10 
Step-down: 3 × 10 
RDL: 3 × 8–10 
Single-leg leg press: 3 × 10 
Hamstring curl: 3 × 10 
Calf raises: 3 × 15 
Lateral band walks: 3 × 10–15 steps each direction 
Single-leg balance with controlled trunk rotation. 
Cardio: bike/elliptical 25–35 min.
If the knee has full ROM, essentially no swelling and good single-leg control, introduce brisk walking → short straight-line jogging intervals if desired. Running isn't necessary for golf, however, so I wouldn't make running a prerequisite.
Golf: Progress from ½ swings → ¾ swings, wedges through mid-irons. Gradually increase rotation and weight transfer. Consider hitting from a tee initially to reduce ground interaction.

Week 5 — Athletic strength + golf-specific loading
Goal: Make the knee tolerate forces greater than those it will ordinarily encounter during golf.
Strength 3 days/week
Squat: 3 × 6–10 
Leg press: 3 × 8–10 
Bulgarian/split squat: 3 × 8 
Single-leg RDL: 3 × 8–10 
Step-down: 3 × 10 
Hamstring curl: 3 × 10 
Lateral band walks: 3 sets 
Single-leg balance with rotation: 3 sets 
Begin controlled dynamic work:
Small lateral steps 
Controlled lateral lunges—only if completely pain-free 
Low-level hops 
Forward/backward movement 
Gradually faster rotational drills 
Golf: Progress to 75–90% swings, longer irons → hybrids/fairway woods → driver.
A sample range session might be:
10 chips → 10 wedges → 10 short irons → 10 mid-irons → 5–10 woods → 5 drivers.
The knee should remain comfortable during the session and later that day/the following morning.

Week 6 — Return to golf
Goal: Demonstrate that the knee can tolerate normal golf without a reaction.
Strength continues 2–3×/week, now approaching normal training loads.
Add more challenging:
Single-leg squat 
Loaded split squat 
Single-leg RDL 
Lateral lunge 
Balance + rotation 
Controlled hopping/agility if appropriate 
Golf progression:
Full range session → 9 holes with cart → 18 holes with cart → 9 holes walking → eventually 18 holes walking.
Full swings can progress toward 100% speed.
I would initially avoid hitting huge buckets of balls. Fifty quality swings with no symptoms tells you more than hitting 150 balls and discovering that evening that the MCL was overloaded.
The most important rule
Use a 24-hour response test. A little discomfort during rehabilitation isn't necessarily a problem, but the knee should be back to baseline by the following morning.
If an exercise or golf session causes increased medial pain, swelling, limping or instability that persists into the next day, reduce the previous workload by roughly 25–50% rather than continuing to progress.
By the end of Week 6, I'd want to see full ROM, no meaningful swelling, no giving-way, pain-free walking/stairs, strong single-leg squat/step-down mechanics, and ideally ≥90% strength/function versus the uninvolved leg before unrestricted sports activity.
