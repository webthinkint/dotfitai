More info:
Constipation is one of the most common non-serious side effects in people using GLP-1 receptor agonists (such as semaglutide or tirzepatide). It usually occurs because these drugs slow gastric emptying and overall intestinal motility, reduce appetite (so total food volume drops), and many users unintentionally reduce fiber, fluid, and magnesium intake while dieting. Practical Recommendation
For most GLP-1 users with mild constipation:
Make sure they are taking a complete multivitamin and mineral formula (MVM) and enough protein (1gm/pound/LBM/D)
Drink 3 L water daily
Add 5–10 g psyllium fiber
Take 200–400 mg magnesium at night
Ensure 20–30 g fiber/day from foods
Walk after meals
This simple protocol resolves the issue for the majority of users.
 


Constipation is one of the most common non-serious side effects in people using GLP-1 receptor agonists (such as semaglutide or tirzepatide). It usually occurs because these drugs slow gastric emptying and overall intestinal motility, reduce appetite (so total food volume drops), and many users unintentionally reduce fiber, fluid, and magnesium intake while dieting. If the issue is not clinical gastroparesis, the goal is simply to restore normal stool volume, hydration, and gut motility.
Below is a practical evidence-based stepwise approach that trainers, clinicians, or users can follow.

1. Hydration First (Most Common Missing Piece)
Many GLP-1 users dramatically reduce food intake and forget to increase fluids.
Recommendation
2.5–3.5 L fluid/day
Add electrolytes or a pinch of sodium to improve absorption
Drink 1–2 glasses of water immediately upon waking
Adequate hydration softens stool and helps fiber work properly.

2. Add Soluble Fiber (Primary Non-Drug Strategy)
GLP-1 diets often become low-fiber because people eat less food overall.
Best evidence-based fibers
Psyllium husk: 5–10 g/day
Partially hydrolyzed guar gum (PHGG): 5–6 g/day
Inulin or resistant starch: 3–8 g/day
Benefits
Increases stool bulk
Improves gut microbiome
Supports metabolic health
Important: fiber must be combined with water.

3. Magnesium for Motility
Many Americans already have suboptimal magnesium intake, and magnesium can help draw water into the colon.
Common options
Magnesium citrate: 200–400 mg at night
Magnesium glycinate: 200–400 mg/day (gentler but less laxative)
Magnesium oxide: stronger laxative effect
Magnesium is often the simplest effective solution.

4. Maintain Adequate Food Volume
GLP-1 users sometimes eat so little that there simply isn’t enough bulk to stimulate bowel movements.
Encourage daily intake of:
Vegetables
Fruit
Whole grains or resistant starch
Protein foods
Aiming for 20–30 g fiber/day total is a good target.

5. Gut Motility Support (Optional)
Certain foods and supplements can help stimulate intestinal movement.
Helpful options:
Kiwi fruit (2/day) – strong evidence for constipation relief
Prunes or prune juice
Coffee – stimulates colon motility
Light physical activity after meals

6. Probiotics / Prebiotics
GLP-1 therapy can alter the microbiome.
Evidence-supported strains for constipation:
Bifidobacterium lactis
Lactobacillus casei
These may improve stool frequency and consistency.

7. If Needed: Gentle OTC Options
Occasional use if lifestyle measures are insufficient:
Polyethylene glycol (Miralax)
Docusate sodium (stool softener)
These are generally safe but should not replace diet and hydration strategies.

Warning Signs (Seek Medical Evaluation)
Constipation with GLP-1 medications should be evaluated if accompanied by:
Severe abdominal pain
Persistent vomiting
Inability to pass stool or gas
Signs of gastroparesis
