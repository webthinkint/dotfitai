Ashwagandha recall: check the lot number — Oct. 30. Vitality Works recalled 365 Whole Foods Market Standardized + Full Spectrum Blend Ashwagandha, 120-count, lot 2025-26227, for potential Salmonella contamination. No illnesses were reported in the notice. Anyone with this lot should stop using it and discard it, rather than return it; refunds are available through Whole Foods. This is a specific product/lot recall. FDA notice
Plant-based diets: the replacement foods matter — Oct. 31. A study of 235 Swedish people aged 16–24 found fortified dairy alternatives contributed substantially to vitamin D, B12 and calcium intake in vegan diets. Some food-only nutrient intakes were nevertheless lower in vegan/vegetarian groups, while fiber was higher and saturated fat lower. This was observational, based on dietary recalls, and excluded supplements from the main analysis: lower food intake does not establish deficiency. Trainer takeaway: assess actual foods and fortification labels, not just the diet’s name. Original study
Healthy aging: muscle size doesn’t capture the whole strength picture — Oct. 31. In a comparison of 22 older and 18 younger men, the older group had 39% lower maximal leg-extension strength and roughly 49–55% lower rapid force development, despite no statistically significant difference in estimated thigh size. This small, cross-sectional study cannot show an individual’s aging trajectory or prove a training effect. Trainer takeaway: track strength and functional performance alongside body composition. Neal, a shorter November 2 roundup today: one new nutrition trial and one current fitness headline worth checking.
Monk-fruit blends: useful sugar-replacement evidence, with tolerance caveats. Published October 31, a randomized crossover trial in 30 healthy adults found that three monk-fruit blends containing fructooligosaccharides produced lower overall glucose and insulin responses than sugar during three-hour testing. Appetite and food intake did not differ, and mild diarrhea was more frequent with the blends. This supports these specific formulations as alternatives to sugar; it does not establish long-term weight-loss benefits or apply automatically to every monk-fruit product. Assessment is based on the publisher’s abstract; full text was paywalled. Trainer/dotFIT takeaway: Consider the complete sweetener blend and digestive tolerance, not just the “natural” label. Online claim check: the “best workout after 55” framing is overhyped; the exercise findings are useful. An October 27 headline says “Not walking or strength training,” highlighting sensorimotor training. The underlying study was published May 29, 2025, not newly released. Across 153 adults and 24 weeks, sensorimotor and aquatic programs improved several physical-function measures. However, there was no dedicated walking or conventional resistance-training comparison group, so the study cannot establish superiority over those activities. Small, uneven groups and predominantly female participants also limit generalization. Trainer takeaway: Balance and coordination work can enrich older-adult programming; this paper gives no reason to replace walking or progressive strength training. Current coverage · Original study
