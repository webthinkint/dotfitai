When you see "Contains a Bioengineered Food Ingredient" or similar wording on a protein bar, it does not mean the protein itself was genetically engineered or that the product was created in a laboratory. In most cases, it means that one or more ingredients are derived from crops that are commonly produced using biotechnology (genetic engineering).
Common examples in protein bars include:
Soy protein or soy lecithin (from bioengineered soybeans) 
Corn-derived ingredients (fiber syrups, maltodextrin, soluble corn fiber, etc.) 
Sugar from sugar beets 
Canola oil 
In the United States, the disclosure is required under the USDA's National Bioengineered Food Disclosure Standard when certain ingredients are derived from bioengineered crops. The label is a transparency requirement, not a safety warning.
For example:
A whey protein bar may carry a bioengineered disclosure simply because it contains soy lecithin used as an emulsifier. 
A plant-based protein bar may carry the disclosure because it contains soy protein from bioengineered soybeans. 
Two otherwise similar bars may differ, with one labeled and one not, depending on ingredient sourcing and supply-chain documentation. 
From a nutrition standpoint, the presence of a bioengineered ingredient does not automatically make the bar higher or lower in protein quality, calories, sugar, or overall healthfulness. For most consumers, factors such as total protein content, essential amino acid profile, fiber, added sugar, and overall ingredient quality are much more relevant than whether the product carries a bioengineered disclosure.
A simple explanation for clients would be:
"The bioengineered label means one or more ingredients in the bar came from crops such as soy, corn, canola, or sugar beets that were produced using modern biotechnology. It's a required disclosure for transparency, not a safety warning, and it doesn't tell you anything about the protein quality or effectiveness of the bar."
