Is folic acid an ok form of folate in multivitamins? Is folic acid bad? 
No, folic acid is good and a common form of folate used in fortification and multivitamin and mineral formulas (MVM) . And don’t be swayed by companies or influencers pushing folate supplements. Folic acid is safe, and it’s the only form of folate that has been shown to prevent neural tube birth defects. Folic acid has been used safely and effectively for decades.
The other form common in MVM is L-5 methyl tetrahydrofolate (L-methylfolate) that may be advantages in certain circumstances because it is already in the biologically active form and bypasses the need for conversion by the MTHFR gene enzyme, which has reduced activity in some individuals. Therefore, it may be preferable for people with certain genetic variants or medical conditions affecting folate metabolism. But it's not as common as the internet would have you believe, and for most people, their body does a perfectly adequate job of converting folic acid.
Bottom line
Folic acid is absolutely a form of folate. It is a synthetic, stable, and effective form that has been used safely for decades. However, L-methylfolate is the active form that the body ultimately uses, so many premium multivitamins choose it because it eliminates the need for metabolic conversion and provides a more direct source of usable folate. For most healthy adults taking a standard multivitamin, either form is effective, although L-methylfolate is generally considered the more advanced formulation choice.

Now here's the important nuance people get wrong:
When someone says "I want a fully methylated multivitamin" — look, I get it, I hear that phrase all the time. But here's the reality: there are only two nutrients in a multivitamin that can actually be in methylated form. That's folate (as methylfolate) and B12 (as methylcobalamin). That's it. Anyone selling you a "fully methylated multi" with a dozen methylated ingredients is either confused or marketing to your confusion. It's literally impossible, not to mention, the product would be useless.

On the B12 front, people will often ask, is there anything truly wrong with using cyanocobalamin as a form of B12 in a multi? And that’s because the same disruptive influencers are selling something using flawed science and context. 
The real answer is: 
Cyanocobalamin is a safe, effective, and well-researched form of vitamin B12 that has been used successfully in multivitamins for decades. Although it must be converted by the body into the active forms—methylcobalamin and adenosylcobalamin—healthy individuals generally perform this conversion efficiently, making it an excellent choice for preventing and correcting B12 deficiency. While premium formulations often use active forms because they require no conversion and align with a "best available ingredients" philosophy, current evidence shows little meaningful clinical advantage over cyanocobalamin for most healthy adults. The small amount of cyanide associated with cyanocobalamin is negligible, naturally detoxified by the body, and not considered a safety concern especially considering the amount is far less than what people ingest naturally from healthy foods such as almonds, flaxseed, lima beans, and cruciferous vegetables. Overall, cyanocobalamin remains a scientifically sound, stable, and reliable source of vitamin B12, while active forms may offer theoretical or niche benefits for certain individuals or premium product positioning. This is why many premium formulas use both forms.
Takeaway:
At the end of the day, does any of this matter if your multi doesn't even have the 20 known potentially under-consumed nutrients covered first? Absolutely not — get your baseline right before splitting hairs on “methylation”
Focus on a complete, third-party tested multi that covers your nutritional bases. The form of folate is refinement, a good one — but it's not the hill to die on if everything else in your multi is solid.
