

Here’s the breakdown based on metabolic studies:
🔬 Resting Energy Expenditure per Tissue Type
| Tissue Type | Approx. Calories Burned per Pound per Day | Relative Activity |
| Skeletal Muscle | ~6 kcal/lb/day | Moderate |
| Liver | ~90 kcal/lb/day | Very high |
| Brain | ~110 kcal/lb/day | Extremely high |
| Heart | ~200 kcal/lb/day | Extremely high |
| Kidneys | ~200 kcal/lb/day | Extremely high |
| Adipose Tissue (Fat) | ~2 kcal/lb/day | Very low |
⚙️ Explanation
Adipose tissue is mostly energy storage, not an active tissue like muscle. However, it does have some metabolic cost — mostly from maintaining fat cell integrity, hormone signaling (like leptin and adiponectin), and low-level turnover of triglycerides.
So for example:
A person with 30 lb of fat would burn roughly 60 kcal/day from that fat mass.
In contrast, 30 lb of muscle would burn around 180 kcal/day.
🧠 Key Takeaway
Fat is metabolically active, but only about one-third as much as muscle and a fraction of the activity of organs.
The main determinant of your resting metabolic rate (RMR) is your lean body mass (LBM), not fat mass.
