Tesamorelin is FDA-approved for only one indication:
Reduction of excess abdominal visceral adipose tissue (VAT) in adults with HIV-associated lipodystrophy. It was approved under the brand name Egrifta. 
What it is
Tesamorelin is a synthetic analog of growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH). It stimulates the pituitary gland to increase natural growth hormone (GH) secretion, which subsequently raises IGF-1 levels and promotes lipolysis (fat breakdown).
What it is NOT FDA-approved for
Tesamorelin is not FDA-approved for:
General weight loss 
Obesity treatment 
Athletic performance enhancement 
Muscle building in healthy individuals 
Anti-aging therapy 
Body recomposition in the general population 
Why people use it off-label
Outside its approved indication, clinicians sometimes prescribe tesamorelin off-label for:
Reduction of visceral fat in non-HIV individuals 
Improvement of body composition 
Support during age-related declines in GH secretion 
Potential preservation of lean mass during fat-loss phases 
However, these uses are based on emerging research and clinical experience rather than FDA-approved indications.
Trainer-level takeaway
Primary Use: Increases natural growth hormone release and reduces visceral abdominal fat. FDA-approved specifically for reducing excess visceral fat in adults with HIV-associated lipodystrophy.
Mechanism: Acts as a potent GHRH analog that binds pituitary GHRH receptors, increasing pulsatile GH secretion, which elevates IGF-1 and promotes preferential mobilization of visceral fat stores.

How Tesamorelin Is Typically Used
1️⃣ Systemic (Non-Site-Specific) Use
This is the standard and intended application
Used for:
Growth hormone axis stimulation
Reduction of visceral adipose tissue
Support of lean body mass retention
Metabolic and lipolytic signaling
Sleep-related GH pulse enhancement
Rationale:
Tesamorelin stimulates the pituitary gland to release GH
Effects are systemic, not localized
Mimics physiological GH pulsatility more closely than exogenous GH
Injection is never site-specific for tissue repair, unlike BPC-157.

Typical Injection Frequency
Once daily is standard
Usually administered in the evening to align with natural GH rhythms
Consistency (same time daily) is emphasized over higher dosing
Commonly Cited Dose Ranges ((may vary in syringe volume with methods of reconstitution – follow supplier directions)
These reflect approved clinical use and common off-label protocols, not trainer-directed recommendations.
2 mg once daily is the FDA-approved dose (for HIV-associated lipodystrophy)
Off-label longevity / body composition protocols often range:
1–2 mg per day
Lower doses (<1 mg/day) generally show less reliable GH elevation
Unlike many peptides, Tesamorelin dosing is not usually split.

Typical Duration of Use
12–26 weeks is common in clinical and applied settings
Some protocols extend to 6–12 months under medical supervision
Effects on visceral fat and IGF-1 are gradual, not immediate
Tesamorelin is not a short-cycle peptide like BPC-157.

Mechanism of Action (Trainer-Appropriate Depth)
Tesamorelin works by:
Stimulating endogenous GH release from the pituitary
Increasing IGF-1 downstream signaling
Enhancing lipolysis, particularly in visceral fat
Preserving natural feedback loops (unlike exogenous GH)
This preserves:
Pulsatile GH secretion
Lower risk of GH suppression
Reduced likelihood of insulin resistance compared to GH injections

Safety & Regulatory Notes (Important)
Tesamorelin is FDA-approved, but only for HIV-associated lipodystrophy
Off-label use requires medical oversight
Possible side effects include:

Joint stiffness
Increased IGF-1
Not appropriate for:

Update product/formula
Tesamorelin is a synthetic Growth Hormone Releasing Hormone (GHRH) analog primarily used to stimulate the body’s natural pulsatile growth hormone release, which subsequently raises IGF-1 levels. Its best-studied and FDA-approved use is the reduction of excess visceral abdominal fat in HIV-associated lipodystrophy. 
The standard clinically studied and FDA-approved dosage is:
2 mg subcutaneous injection once daily 
The newer Egrifta WR formulation is dosed at:
1.28 mg once daily due to formulation differences and bioequivalence improvements. 
Mechanistically, tesamorelin works upstream at the pituitary to increase endogenous GH secretion, which then elevates IGF-1 and promotes lipolysis, especially in visceral adipose tissue:

Clinical trials using the 2 mg/day dose demonstrated:
~14–18% reductions in visceral abdominal fat, 
improvements in triglycerides and liver fat, 
preservation or modest increases in lean body mass, 
while having minimal effect on overall body weight. 
In off-label performance/anti-aging settings, many clinics still use dosing near the original 2 mg nightly range because that is where most human efficacy data exist. Some protocols use lower doses (e.g., 0.5–1 mg/day) to reduce side effects or cost, but these lower-dose approaches are far less validated in published human research.
The most common side effects reported include:
edema/water retention, 
joint pain, 
tingling or numbness, 
injection site reactions, 
and possible insulin sensitivity changes.
