Which Peptide Fits Which Client Goal?
Trainer / Educator Decision Flow (Educational Use Only)




START HERE
➜ Identify the client’s PRIMARY GOAL
(not symptoms, not expectations)

🟦 GOAL #1: Sleep • Recovery • Healthy Aging
Poor sleep quality
Slower recovery from training
Wants to preserve lean mass with age
Comfortable with gradual, long-term benefits
⬇
✅ Sermorelin
Why:
• Stimulates natural GH release
• Preserves physiologic hormone rhythms
• Best suited for longevity & recovery support
Trainer framing:
“Supports how your body naturally produces growth hormone over time.”

🟩 GOAL #2: Visceral Fat • Metabolic Health
Central / abdominal fat concern
Cardiometabolic risk awareness
Already training & eating consistently
Wants evidence-supported outcomes
⬇
✅ Tesamorelin
Why:
• Strong GH signaling
• Unique evidence for visceral fat reduction
• Metabolic-focused application
Trainer framing:
“Used when fat distribution and metabolic health are the main focus.”

🟨 GOAL #3: Performance • Physique • Training Output
Already lean and well-trained
Wants improved recovery between sessions
Interested in advanced performance tools
Understands results are incremental
⬇
✅ CJC-1295
Why:
• Enhances GH pulse amplitude & duration
• Often discussed in performance settings
• Requires strong lifestyle foundation
Trainer framing:
“A performance-oriented option—not a beginner tool.”

🟥 GOAL #4: Fast, Dramatic, Guaranteed Results
Wants rapid fat loss or muscle gain
Looking for “strongest” or “quickest” solution
Less invested in fundamentals
⬇
❌ NO PEPTIDE DISCUSSION
Why:
• Expectations misaligned
• High risk of disappointment
• Basics not yet mastered
Trainer framing:
“Advanced tools only work when the foundation is solid.”

⚕️ GOAL #5: Diagnosed GH Deficiency
Physician-confirmed deficiency
Endocrinologist involved
Medical hormone replacement indicated
⬇
🏥 Growth Hormone (GH)
Important distinction:
• GH = medical hormone replacement
• Not a wellness or fitness peptide
• Requires physician management
Trainer framing:
“That’s medical therapy, not a training supplement.”

Trainer Guardrails (Footer)
Peptides are experimental adjuncts
They do not replace:
Resistance training
Adequate protein & EAAs
Micronutrient sufficiency
Sleep & recovery hygiene
Trainers do not prescribe, dose, or administer

Trainer Close (Bold Call-Out)
“The right peptide enhances a strong foundation—it never replaces one.”
