oes bacteriostatic water need to be refrigerated if not been opened yet - i.e., just stored before use 
In general, no. Unopened bacteriostatic water does not need to be refrigerated.
Here are the standard storage recommendations:
Before opening: Store at controlled room temperature, typically 20-25°C (68-77°F), with brief excursions generally allowed between 15-30°C (59-86°F) according to most manufacturers. 
Keep it in its original carton or otherwise protected from direct light. 
Do not freeze it. 
Avoid prolonged exposure to excessive heat (e.g., leaving it in a hot car). 
After opening
Once the vial has been punctured:
It can still be stored at room temperature unless the manufacturer specifically recommends refrigeration. 
Some people choose to refrigerate it because it may be more comfortable when used for reconstitution and may reduce bacterial growth slightly, but refrigeration is not generally required for bacteriostatic water containing 0.9% benzyl alcohol as the preservative. 
The preservative allows the vial to be used for multiple withdrawals, but most manufacturers recommend discarding it 28 days after the first puncture, even if solution remains. 
If you're using it to reconstitute peptides
After mixing a peptide with bacteriostatic water, the peptide's storage requirements become the priority, not the water's. Most common peptide solutions (such as tesamorelin, BPC-157, TB-500, or IGF-1 LR3) are generally stored refrigerated at 2-8°C (36-46°F) after reconstitution to help maintain stability, although specific recommendations vary by product and manufacturer.
So, for your situation—an unopened vial being stored until needed—a cool, dry cabinet at room temperature is the appropriate place.
