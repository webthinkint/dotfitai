Epitalon, a synthetic tetrapeptide (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly), operates through multiple mechanisms at the cellular and genetic levels, most notably by stimulating the enzyme telomerase to increase telomere length. It also functions as an antioxidant, regulates the endocrine system (specifically melatonin production), and modulates gene expression. 
Key mechanisms of action include:
Telomerase Activation and Telomere Elongation: Epitalon induces the expression and activity of the telomerase enzyme, which adds repetitive DNA sequences (telomeres) to the ends of chromosomes. This action helps prevent telomere shortening, a key biomarker of aging, thereby extending the proliferative capacity and lifespan of normal somatic cells in in vitro studies.
Melatonin Secretion Regulation: The peptide acts on the pineal gland to restore and normalize age-related declines in melatonin production, which in turn helps regulate circadian rhythms and improve sleep patterns.
Antioxidant Effects: Epitalon acts as a potent antioxidant, directly scavenging reactive oxygen species (free radicals) and also stimulating the expression of endogenous antioxidant enzymes (like superoxide dismutase). This activity reduces oxidative stress and cellular damage associated with aging and age-related diseases.
Gene Expression Modulation (Epigenetic Mechanism): Epitalon has been shown to interact with histone proteins in chromatin, an epigenetic mechanism that can influence the transcription of various genes, including those involved in cellular differentiation, neurogenesis, immune function, and the suppression of tumor formation.
Immune System Modulation: Research indicates that epitalon can help regulate immune function by influencing mediators such as cytokines and enhancing immune cell activity, potentially counteracting age-related immune system decline. 
Through these interconnected mechanisms, epitalon contributes to overall cellular health, tissue repair, and the potential delay of age-related diseases, as demonstrated in various preclinical and some early human studies
