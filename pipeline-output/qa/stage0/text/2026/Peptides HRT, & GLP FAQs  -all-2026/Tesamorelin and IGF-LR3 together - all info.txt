Mindful that a person is healthy, eating well, exercising, taking a complete multivitamin and getting enough protein, is taking the peptide IGF/LR3 along with Tesamorelin a good program for strength and generally considered safe. And what would be the dosages.
For a healthy adult who is already doing the fundamentals well (resistance training, adequate protein, sufficient calories, complete micronutrition, sleep, and recovery), the question becomes whether adding Tesamorelin and IGF-1 LR3 provides enough additional benefit to justify the cost, uncertainty, and potential risks.
Short Answer
Tesamorelin has the stronger clinical foundation. It is FDA-approved for reducing visceral adipose tissue in HIV-associated lipodystrophy and reliably increases endogenous growth hormone and IGF-1 levels. It may modestly improve body composition and recovery in some individuals, but it is not approved for strength enhancement. 
IGF-1 LR3 is almost entirely an experimental/research peptide. There is very little high-quality human data supporting its use for muscle growth or strength in healthy adults. Most claims come from animal studies, mechanistic theory, or anecdotal reports. 
From an evidence standpoint, if someone asked me which is the more defensible intervention, Tesamorelin is far ahead of IGF-1 LR3.

Potential Benefits
Tesamorelin
Mechanism:
Stimulates the pituitary to release growth hormone 
Raises endogenous IGF-1 
May increase protein synthesis indirectly 
Often reduces visceral fat 
Possible outcomes:
Improved body composition 
Modest lean mass increases 
Improved recovery 
Reduced abdominal visceral fat 
IGF-1 LR3
Mechanism:
Directly stimulates IGF-1 receptors 
Activates anabolic signaling pathways (AKT/mTOR) 
May increase satellite cell activity and muscle protein synthesis 
Possible outcomes reported anecdotally:
Increased muscle fullness 
Increased training recovery 
Improved nutrient partitioning 
Modest hypertrophy 
However, these outcomes remain largely unproven in controlled human studies.

Safety Considerations
Tesamorelin
Generally considered reasonably safe under physician supervision.
Common adverse effects:
Water retention 
Joint discomfort 
Edema 
Increased fasting glucose 
Insulin resistance in susceptible individuals 
Monitoring:
IGF-1 
Fasting glucose 
HbA1c 
Lipids 
IGF-1 LR3
This is where concerns increase.
Potential risks:
Hypoglycemia 
Edema 
Headaches 
Carpal tunnel symptoms 
Insulin resistance with chronic use 
Theoretical concerns regarding stimulation of pre-existing cancer cells 
IGF-1 is a potent growth factor. While there is no evidence that therapeutic use causes cancer, elevated IGF-1 levels have long been associated epidemiologically with increased risk of certain cancers.
This is one reason many endocrinologists are cautious regarding long-term supraphysiologic IGF-1 exposure.

Relative Strength Benefits
If the goal is strength and muscle gain, evidence would generally rank interventions roughly like this:
Progressive resistance training 
Adequate calories and protein 
Creatine monohydrate 
Optimized sleep and recovery 
Testosterone replacement (if clinically deficient) 
Growth hormone secretagogues (Tesamorelin, Sermorelin) 
IGF-1 LR3 
In other words, IGF-1 LR3 is often discussed as a powerful anabolic agent, but the actual human evidence is surprisingly thin.

Typical Research/Clinical Doses
Tesamorelin
Clinical dose:

Injected subcutaneously once daily 
Usually administered before bedtime 
FDA-approved dosing is 2 mg/day 
IGF-1 LR3
Common research-community usage:

Some users report:
20–40 mcg/day conservatively 
40–80 mcg/day aggressively 
Cycles commonly discussed:
4–8 weeks 
There is no medically established dosing protocol for healthy adults because it is not an approved therapeutic drug.

My Evidence-Based Assessment
For a healthy, well-trained adult:
Tesamorelin
Reasonable evidence for body-composition improvement 
Generally manageable safety profile with monitoring 
Most defensible peptide of the two 
IGF-1 LR3
Theoretical anabolic benefits 
Weak human evidence 
Greater uncertainty regarding long-term safety 
Much harder to justify from a risk/benefit perspective 
If someone already has:
Excellent nutrition 
Adequate protein 
Creatine supplementation 
Proper resistance training 
Good sleep 
then Tesamorelin may provide modest additional body-composition benefits. IGF-1 LR3 is far less certain and is generally considered a much more experimental approach.
The biggest practical issue I see in real-world users is that expectations are often much larger than the actual measurable improvements, particularly compared with what can still be achieved through optimizing training volume, recovery, and protein intake.
would using both simultaneously be ok in the same cycle
From a purely pharmacologic standpoint, Tesamorelin and IGF-1 LR3 are not directly contraindicated together, because they work at different points in the growth hormone/IGF axis:
Tesamorelin → stimulates your pituitary to release more growth hormone → which then increases your body's own IGF-1 production. 
IGF-1 LR3 → bypasses that pathway and directly activates IGF-1 receptors. 
So, mechanistically, they can have additive effects.
However, the main concern is that both ultimately increase total IGF signaling, which is where most of the potential risks come from.
Potential Advantages of Combining
The theoretical rationale is:
Tesamorelin raises physiologic GH pulses. 
GH may improve recovery, connective tissue support, and fat metabolism. 
IGF-1 LR3 provides more direct anabolic signaling. 
Some users report:
Better recovery 
Improved training volume tolerance 
Increased muscle fullness 
Improved body composition 
But again, there is very little controlled human research evaluating the combination in healthy adults.

Potential Concerns
The biggest concerns when combining are:
1. Excessive IGF-1 Exposure
Tesamorelin already elevates circulating IGF-1.
Adding IGF-1 LR3 on top can push total IGF signaling higher than either alone.
Potential issues:
Edema 
Joint discomfort 
Carpal tunnel symptoms 
Headaches 
Increased insulin resistance 
Theoretical increased risk of stimulating growth of existing tumors 
2. Glucose Regulation
GH and IGF pathways interact with insulin sensitivity.
Monitoring:
Fasting glucose 
HbA1c 
Fasting insulin (if available) 
becomes more important when stacking compounds.
3. Diminishing Returns
Many people assume:
Tesamorelin = 10 
IGF-LR3 = 10 
Combined = 20 
Biology rarely works that way.
Often you get:
More cost 
More risk 
Smaller incremental benefit than expected 

What Would I Consider the More Rational Approach?
If the goal is:
Better body composition 
Recovery 
Long-term use 
I would generally view:
Tesamorelin alone
as the more evidence-supported option.
Then evaluate:
IGF-1 levels 
Body composition changes 
Recovery 
Strength progression 
before adding another compound.

Monitoring I'd Want to See
If someone were using both under physician supervision, I'd strongly recommend periodic monitoring of:
IGF-1 
Fasting glucose 
HbA1c 
CMP (liver/kidney function) 
Lipid panel 
and stopping if IGF-1 rises substantially above the laboratory reference range.

My Overall Assessment
For a healthy, resistance-trained adult:
Using both simultaneously is not inherently irrational from a mechanistic standpoint. 
It is commonly discussed in peptide and bodybuilding circles. 
The combination is much less studied than either alone, particularly in healthy adults. 
The largest concern is cumulative IGF exposure and metabolic side effects rather than a specific drug-drug interaction. 
If the objective is maximizing long-term health, strength, and body composition, I would be considerably more comfortable with a monitored Tesamorelin protocol than with chronic combined Tesamorelin + IGF-1 LR3 use, simply because the safety and efficacy evidence is much stronger for Tesamorelin.
