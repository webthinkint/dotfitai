Kisspeptin works by binding to its receptor on neurons in the hypothalamus, which stimulates the release of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH). This begins a cascade: GnRH then signals the pituitary gland to release luteinizing hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), which in turn stimulate the gonads (ovaries or testes) to produce sex hormones like estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone. This process is crucial for reproduction, puberty, and the menstrual cycle.  
The chain of command
Kisspeptin starts the signal: Kisspeptin is a peptide hormone that acts on neurons in the hypothalamus. 
GnRH is released: When kisspeptin binds to the KISS1R receptor on these neurons, it triggers the release of GnRH. 
LH and FSH are released: GnRH travels to the pituitary gland, which then releases LH and FSH. 
Sex hormones are produced: LH and FSH travel to the ovaries or testes, stimulating them to produce and release sex hormones such as estrogen, progesterone, and testosterone. 
Key roles in reproduction
Puberty: 
Kisspeptin signaling is essential for the onset of puberty. 
Menstrual cycle: 
It helps regulate the release of eggs from the ovary and is involved in the normal control of ovulation. 
Fertility: 
It is a key regulator of the reproductive axis and is essential for fertility. 
Other functions: 
Kisspeptin is also involved in a variety of other processes, including the integration of reproductive hormones with brain activity related to emotions and mood
