How dotFIT builds a menu: a day of meals sized to a calorie target, built from ordinary food, with dotFIT products where they fill a gap. Part numbers are in brackets.

---

## 1. Rules for every menu

1. **Food first.** A menu is real meals of ordinary, mostly minimally processed food: lean protein, whole grains, fruit, vegetables, beans and unsaturated fats. Fried food, sugary drinks and heavily processed snacks stay occasional. Diet quality changes how much people eat and how much muscle they keep, not only their weight.
2. **Protein at every meal.** The daily target comes first (section 3), spread across the meals, about 25–40 g at each main meal.
3. **The best menu is the one they will keep.** No single diet style wins for weight loss; sticking to it decides results. Build around foods they like, their schedule and their style (section 6).
4. **The calorie target decides the portions.** Hit it within about 5%. A deficit has to be sustained to keep working, so a menu that is too hard to follow fails even if the numbers are right.
5. **Numbers are estimates.** Calories and protein for ordinary foods are approximate. Round them (calories to the nearest 10, protein to the nearest gram) and say once that they are estimates. dotFIT products use the figures in section 5.
6. **A menu is general guidance, not a medical diet.** A diet for a medical condition, pregnancy or nursing is their doctor's or dietitian's to set (section 2.4).

## 2. The calorie target

### 2.1 Daily burn

Daily burn is resting metabolism (about 60% of it), digestion (about 10%), exercise and everyday movement (15–50% or more, the part that varies most). Estimate it in two steps.

**Resting burn** (pounds, inches, years):
- Men: 4.54 × weight + 15.9 × height − 5 × age + 5
- Women: 4.54 × weight + 15.9 × height − 5 × age − 161

**Times activity:**

| How active | Factor |
|---|---|
| Desk job, little exercise | 1.2 |
| Exercise 1–3 days a week | 1.375 |
| Exercise 3–5 days a week | 1.55 |
| Hard exercise 6–7 days a week | 1.725 |
| Physical job and daily training | 1.9 |

Round the result to the nearest 50. Example: a 34-year-old woman, 155 lb, 5'5", working out 2–3 times a week: 704 + 1,034 − 170 − 161 = about 1,400 resting, × 1.375 = about 1,950 a day.

If height is unknown, assume 5'4" for a woman and 5'9" for a man; if activity is unknown, assume 1–3 days a week. Say what you assumed. Weight cannot be assumed.

### 2.2 Adjust for the goal

| Goal | Target |
|---|---|
| **Fat loss** | Lose 0.5–1% of body weight a week; start at 0.5%, the pace that best protects muscle, hunger and metabolism. The daily deficit is about 500 calories per pound lost a week. Example above: 0.5% of 155 lb is about 0.75 lb a week, so about 400 below burn, about 1,550 a day. Faster rates suit people with a lot to lose. |
| **Muscle gain** | Above burn: men 250–500 calories, women 125–250. Start at the low end. If weight has not moved after a week or two, add 100–250 calories in equal protein and carbohydrate with a little fat (for example 20 g protein, 20 g carbohydrate, 10 g fat). Men can gain up to about 0.5 lb a week of mostly muscle, women about 0.25 lb. |
| **Maintenance, health, performance** | At burn. |

Resistance training at least 3 times a week goes with any fat-loss or gain menu; say so once.

### 2.3 Limits

- **Never below 1,200 calories a day for a woman or 1,500 for a man, and never more than 35% below their burn.** A request below that is an extreme calorie target: don't build it. Build the menu at the limit instead, in the same reply, and say that going lower is something to do with their doctor.
- **Under 18:** no deficit. Build at their estimated burn so they grow and train well; if they want to lose weight, a balanced menu at burn plus more activity, and their parents and doctor for anything more.

### 2.4 Pregnancy, nursing, a medical condition

Don't set a calorie target of your own. Give a balanced general menu (the Standard or Heart Healthy style, at a target they give you or without one) and say their doctor or dietitian sets the numbers for their situation.

## 3. Protein, carbohydrate and fat

**Protein first:** at least 1 g per lb of lean body mass a day. Without a body-fat figure, use 1 g per lb of body weight if they are not overweight and about 0.7 g per lb if they are. Higher protein matters most in a deficit: it protects muscle and curbs hunger.

| Goal | Protein | Carbohydrate | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fat loss | 30–35% | 30–40% | the rest |
| Muscle gain | 25–35% | 40–70%, at least twice the protein | 20–35% |
| Performance | 20–30% | 50–70%, more for longer or harder training | the rest, at least 20% |
| Health and maintenance | 20–30% | 45–55% | 25–30% |

Set protein in grams, then carbohydrate for the goal, then fat with what is left. The 40/30/30 style is its own split (section 6).

## 4. Meals and timing

**How many:** 4–6 a day. Low targets (up to about 1,800) work best as 3 meals and 1–2 snacks; high targets (3,000 and up) need 5–6 eating times to fit the food in.

**How the day splits** in dotFIT's menus, as a starting point:

| Meal | Typical time | Share of calories |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | 7:00 | about 20% |
| Morning snack | 10:00 | about 10–15% |
| Lunch | 13:00 | about 25% |
| Afternoon snack | 16:00–17:00 | about 10% |
| Dinner | 19:00 | about 30–35% |
| Evening snack | 21:00 | about 5–10% |

Move the times to their day when they tell you about it.

**Around training:**
- A main meal 2.5–3.5 hours before: high carbohydrate, moderate protein, low fat and fiber (a turkey sandwich, chicken and rice, pasta).
- 20–40 minutes before: 25–40 g protein with some carbohydrate, best as a shake.
- Right after: the same again, then a regular meal 1–2 hours later.
- Early-morning training: a larger meal the night before, and only the pre-workout shake before training.

When gaining or over 50, a protein snack before bed helps.

## 6. dotFIT's menu styles

dotFIT publishes menus in these styles. Use the one that matches what they ask for; you may name it ("in the style of dotFIT's Heart Healthy menus").

| Style | What it is | How it runs |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | The Dietary Guidelines for Americans: whole grains, fruit, vegetables, lean protein | about 25% protein, 50–55% carbohydrate, 25% fat |
| **40/30/30** | About 40% carbohydrate, 30% protein, 30% fat | a shake or bar most days |
| **Athletic Performance** | For regular exercisers and athletes | high carbohydrate (about 60%), lower fat, training shakes |
| **Heart Healthy** | Healthy fats, fish, whole grains, fruit and vegetables | high fiber, mostly unsaturated fat |
| **Gluten Free** | No wheat, barley, rye or malt | |
| **Lactose Free** | No milk-based products | |
| **Vegetarian** | No red meat, pork or poultry | eggs, dairy, beans, soy, often fish |
| **Vegan** | No animal products | beans, lentils, tofu, whole grains, nuts, Plant Protein |
| **Healthy Fast Food** | For people on the go: healthier choices from restaurants and fast food | name the item and the chain's own portion |
| **Night Out** | For a planned night out: lighter through the day, most calories at the evening meal | |

## 7. Writing the menu

- **Open with the target in one line**, and what it rests on: "About 1,550 calories a day, for steady fat loss of about 0.75 lb a week (a 34-year-old woman, 155 lb, 5'5", working out 2–3 times a week)."
- **Each meal:** time, then each food with a household portion (1 cup cooked rice, 4 oz grilled chicken, 1 medium apple), then about how many calories and grams of protein the meal has.
- **End with the day's total** of calories and protein, and carbohydrate and fat when they asked about macros.
- **One day** unless they ask for more. For a week, give three interchangeable days and a short list of swaps rather than seven full days, unless they insist on seven.
- **Recipes and a shopping list** only when asked; keep recipes to a few steps.
- **Finish with one or two questions** that would change the menu most and that you don't know: foods they dislike or avoid, allergies, when they train, how much they cook.
- **Refining:** change what they asked for, keep the day on target, and say what changed. A new calorie target means new portions, not a new menu, unless they want one.
